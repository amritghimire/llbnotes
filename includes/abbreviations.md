*[jurisprudentia]: Latin: juris = of law, prudentia = knowledge or skill. "Knowledge of law".
*[jurist]: A legal scholar who writes about law (कानूनविद्).
*[positive law]: Law actually laid down by a human authority (a state, parliament or court), as opposed to moral or natural law.
*[natural law]: Law believed to come from nature, reason, morality or God, and to stand above man-made law (प्राकृतिक कानून).
*[general jurisprudence]: Austin: the study of principles common to "the ampler and maturer systems" of law.
*[particular jurisprudence]: Austin: the study of the law of one particular country.
*[expository jurisprudence]: Bentham: explains the law as it is.
*[censorial jurisprudence]: Bentham: judges the law and says what it ought to be.
*[analytical jurisprudence]: Studies the law as it is: its basic ideas and structure (विश्लेषणात्मक विधिशास्त्र).
*[historical jurisprudence]: Studies how law grew over time from custom and the life of a people (ऐतिहासिक विधिशास्त्र).
*[ethical jurisprudence]: Studies what the law ought to be, judged by justice and morality (नैतिक विधिशास्त्र).
*[sociological jurisprudence]: Studies law as a tool that works inside society and shapes it (समाजशास्त्रीय विधिशास्त्र).
*[comparative jurisprudence]: Compares the laws of different countries to find common ideas and better rules.
*[social engineering]: Roscoe Pound's idea that law balances competing interests in society, like an engineer balancing forces.
*[legislation]: Law made by a law-making body such as Parliament (विधायन). An Act made this way is an ऐन.
*[precedent]: An earlier court decision that guides or binds courts in later similar cases (नजिर).
*[custom]: A practice followed so long and so widely that people treat it as binding (प्रथा).
*[ratio decidendi]: Latin: "the reason for the decision". The legal principle a case is decided on; the binding part of a judgment.
*[Muluki Ain]: Nepal's general law code (मुलुकी ऐन), first made in 1910 B.S. (1854 A.D.), re-made in 2020 B.S., replaced by the Muluki Codes of 2074 B.S.
*[B.S]: Bikram Sambat, the Nepali calendar. About 56 years 8 months ahead of A.D.
*[NKP]: Nepal Kanoon Patrika (नेपाल कानून पत्रिका), the official journal of Supreme Court decisions.
*[lex iniusta non est lex]: Latin: "an unjust law is not law". Natural-school idea linked to St Augustine and Aquinas.
*[jus naturale]: Latin: natural law, in Roman law.
*[jus gentium]: Latin: "law of peoples". Roman law common to all nations.
*[jus civile]: Latin: the law of the city of Rome; the law of one state.
*[social contract]: The idea that people created the state by agreement, giving up some freedom for protection (सामाजिक सम्झौता).
*[Volksgeist]: German: "spirit of the people". Savigny's view that law grows from a people's shared consciousness.
*[positivism]: The view that law is what a human authority lays down, kept separate from morality (प्रत्यक्षवाद).
*[command theory]: Austin's theory: law is a command of the sovereign backed by a sanction.
*[sovereign]: Austin: the person or body habitually obeyed by most of society, who habitually obeys no one else (सार्वभौम).
*[sanction]: The penalty that follows breaking a law.
*[positive morality]: Austin's name for rules that are not "law properly so called", e.g. international law, etiquette.
*[Grundnorm]: German: "basic norm". Kelsen's presupposed highest norm from which all legal norms get validity.
*[rule of recognition]: Hart: the rule officials use to identify which rules are valid law.
*[living law]: Ehrlich: the rules people actually follow in society, as opposed to the statute book.
*[legal realism]: The view that law is what courts actually do (कानूनी यथार्थवाद).
*[superstructure]: Marx: law, politics and ideas, built on the economic base of society.
*[dharma]: Hindu concept of a higher moral order and duty (धर्म), binding even on the king.
*[critical legal studies]: A 1970s American movement which says law is not neutral but a form of politics that serves the powerful.
*[indeterminacy]: The CLS claim that legal rules do not fix one right answer, so a judge's values decide the case.
*[legitimation]: Making an unfair system look fair and natural so that people accept it.
*[trashing]: CLS technique of showing that a respected legal doctrine is inconsistent, biased or empty.
*[feminist jurisprudence]: Legal theory that studies how law treats women and how to make it equal (नारीवादी विधिशास्त्र).
*[patriarchy]: A social system in which men hold most power in the family and in public life (पितृसत्ता).
*[intersectionality]: Crenshaw's idea that a person can face several forms of discrimination at once, such as gender and caste.
*[positive discrimination]: Special measures favouring a disadvantaged group so it can reach real equality (सकारात्मक विभेद).
*[substantive equality]: Equality in actual results and conditions, not only equal words in the law.
*[postmodernism]: A late-1900s view that rejects universal truths and grand theories (उत्तरआधुनिकतावाद).
*[metanarratives]: "Grand stories" that claim to explain everything, such as natural law or Marxism. Lyotard's term.
*[deconstruction]: Derrida's method of reading a text to expose its hidden assumptions and contradictions.
*[coparcener]: A family member entitled to a share when family property is partitioned (अंशियार).
*[coparceners]: Family members entitled to shares when family property is partitioned (अंशियार).
*[directive order]: A Supreme Court order telling the government to do something, such as bring a bill (निर्देशनात्मक आदेश).
*[chhaupadi]: The practice of isolating women and girls during menstruation, mainly in western Nepal.
*[CEDAW]: Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination against Women (1979). Nepal ratified it in 1991.
*[FWLD]: Forum for Women, Law and Development, a Nepali NGO that filed many gender-equality cases.
*[PIL]: Public interest litigation: a case filed for the public good, not only for the petitioner's own right (सार्वजनिक सरोकारको मुद्दा).
*[stare decisis]: Latin: "stand by what has been decided". Courts follow earlier decisions on the same point of law.
*[obiter dicta]: Latin: "things said by the way". A judge's remarks not needed for the decision; persuasive only.
*[per incuriam]: Latin: "through lack of care". A decision given in ignorance of a statute or binding case; it does not bind.
*[sub silentio]: Latin: "in silence". A point decided without being argued or considered; weak as precedent.
*[opinio necessitatis]: Latin: the belief that a practice is followed because it is obligatory, not by choice. Needed for a valid custom.
*[responsa prudentium]: Latin: "answers of the learned". Opinions of Roman jurists, a source of Roman law.
*[ultra vires]: Latin: "beyond the powers". An act or rule made outside the authority given; it is void.
*[delegated legislation]: Rules made by the government or another body under power given by an Act (प्रत्यायोजित विधायन).
*[equity]: Fairness used to fill gaps in the law or soften a harsh rule; in England, the rules of the Court of Chancery.
*[niyamawali]: Nepali: rules (नियमावली) made by the government under an Act.
*[time immemorial]: In English law, the year 1189 A.D. A local custom must have existed since then.
*[Court of Record]: A court whose decisions are recorded and can be cited as authority. Nepal's Supreme Court is one (Article 128(2)).
*[abrogative]: Able to repeal or abolish existing law.
*[authoritative precedent]: A precedent a court must follow, such as a decision of a higher court.
*[persuasive precedent]: A precedent a court may follow if convinced, such as a foreign decision.
*[Law of Citations]: A Roman law of 426 A.D. that made the opinions of five jurists binding on judges.
*[guthi]: A Nepali trust-like endowment (गुठी) that funds religious and social activities.
*[justitia]: Latin: justice. From jus, "right" or "law".
*[suum cuique tribuere]: Latin: "to give each his own". The core of Ulpian's definition of justice.
*[original position]: Rawls: an imagined meeting where people choose the basic rules of society.
*[veil of ignorance]: Rawls: choosing rules without knowing your own class, caste, sex or talent, so the rules come out fair.
*[difference principle]: Rawls: inequality is just only if it most benefits the least advantaged.
*[utilitarians]: Thinkers such as Bentham and Mill who judge laws by "the greatest happiness of the greatest number".
*[geometrical proportion]: Aristotle: sharing in proportion to merit (distributive justice).
*[arithmetical proportion]: Aristotle: treating the parties as equals and restoring the balance (corrective justice).
*[rectificatory]: Correcting or putting right. Another name for corrective justice.
*[mens rea]: Latin: "guilty mind". The intention or negligence behind a crime.
*[actus reus]: Latin: "guilty act". The physical act or omission that makes up a crime.
*[lex talionis]: Latin: "law of retaliation". An eye for an eye, a tooth for a tooth.
*[expiatory]: Washing away guilt through suffering or penance.
*[retributive]: Punishing because the offender deserves it, in proportion to the wrong.
*[prayaschitta]: Hindu law: penance or atonement for a sin (प्रायश्चित्त).
*[probation]: Release of an offender under supervision instead of sending him to jail.
*[parole]: Early release from prison on conditions, under a parole officer's supervision.
*[open prison]: A prison with few walls or guards, where trusted prisoners live and work more freely.
*[community service]: Unpaid work for the public that a court orders instead of jail.
*[suspended sentence]: A jail term the court does not carry out unless the offender breaks set conditions.
*[sanctioning right]: Salmond: the right to a remedy that arises when a primary right is broken.
*[primary right]: Salmond: a right that exists by itself, such as the right to be paid a debt.
*[restitution]: Giving back what was wrongly taken or received.
*[injunction]: A court order not to do something (निषेधाज्ञा). An order to do something is a mandatory order (आदेशात्मक आदेश).
*[locus standi]: Latin: "place to stand". The right to bring a case because one has a lawful interest in it.
*[res judicata]: Latin: "a matter already judged". A decided matter cannot be tried again between the same parties.
*[indigent]: Very poor; unable to pay for a lawyer.
*[person of inherence]: Salmond: the owner or holder of a right.
*[person of incidence]: Salmond: the person who bears the duty that matches a right.
*[right in rem]: Latin in rem = "against a thing". A right good against the whole world, such as ownership.
*[right in personam]: Latin in personam = "against a person". A right good only against a definite person, such as a contract claim.
*[in re propria]: Latin: "in one's own thing". A right in one's own property, such as ownership.
*[in re aliena]: Latin: "in another's thing". A right in property owned by someone else, such as a lease or mortgage.
*[encumbrance]: A right held by someone over property owned by another, which limits the owner's right: lease, servitude, security or trust.
*[easement]: A right to make limited use of a neighbour's land, such as a right of way.
*[jural correlatives]: Hohfeld: pairs of legal positions that always go together between two persons, such as right and duty.
*[jural opposites]: Hohfeld: pairs of legal positions where having one means lacking the other, such as privilege and duty.
*[no-right]: Hohfeld: the position of a person who cannot demand that another act or not act; the correlative of a privilege.
*[imperfect right]: A right the law recognises but courts will not enforce, such as a time-barred debt.
*[time-barred]: Too late to sue, because the legal time limit for bringing the claim has passed (हदम्याद नाघेको).
*[limitation period]: The time limit within which a claim must be brought to court (हदम्याद).
*[set-off]: Cancelling a debt you owe against a debt owed to you by the same person.
*[Court of Chancery]: The old English court that applied equity (rules of fairness and conscience) alongside the common law courts.
*[bona fide purchaser]: Latin bona fide = "in good faith". A buyer who pays value and honestly does not know of another's claim.
*[ubi jus ibi remedium]: Latin: "where there is a right, there is a remedy".
*[investitive]: Salmond: describes a fact that creates a right (e.g. a purchase).
*[divestitive]: Salmond: describes a fact that ends a right (e.g. a sale by the owner).
*[will theory]: The theory that a right is the expression of its holder's will or choice (Kant, Hegel, Savigny, Holland).
*[interest theory]: Ihering's theory that a right is a legally protected interest.
*[choice theory]: H.L.A. Hart's modern will theory: the right-holder is the one who can enforce or waive the duty.
*[absolute duties]: Austin: duties with no matching right in any definite person, such as duties owed to the public.
*[relative duties]: Austin: duties that match a right held by a definite person.
*[directive principles]: Policy goals for the state in Part 4 of the Constitution of Nepal; courts cannot enforce them (Article 55).
*[habeas corpus]: Latin: "you shall have the body". A writ ordering that a detained person be brought before the court, to test whether the detention is lawful.
*[mandamus]: Latin: "we command". A writ ordering a public authority to perform its legal duty.
*[certiorari]: A writ quashing an unlawful decision of a lower court or authority.
*[quo warranto]: Latin: "by what authority". A writ asking a person to show by what right he holds a public office.
*[persona]: Latin: "mask" worn by an actor; the root of the legal word "person".
*[persona ficta]: Latin: "fictitious person". A body treated as a person only by legal fiction.
*[legal person]: A person created or recognised by law that is not a human being, such as a company (कानूनी व्यक्ति).
*[natural person]: A living human being as a holder of rights and duties (प्राकृतिक व्यक्ति).
*[corporation sole]: A corporation made of one office-holder at a time, such as the Crown or a bishop. The office outlives the holder.
*[corporation aggregate]: A corporation made of many members at once, such as a company or university.
*[perpetual succession]: The rule that a corporation continues to exist even when its members change or die.
*[body corporate]: A group or office the law treats as one person, separate from its members (संगठित संस्था).
*[ipso facto]: Latin: "by the fact itself". Automatically, without any further step.
*[nasciturus pro iam nato habetur, quotiens de commodo eius agitur]: Latin: a child to be born is treated as already born whenever its benefit is concerned.
*[en ventre sa mère]: Law French: "in its mother's womb". An unborn child.
*[Zweckvermögen]: German: "purpose-property". Property dedicated to a purpose rather than owned by a person (purpose theory).
*[debentures]: Written acknowledgments of a loan to a company, usually secured on its property.
*[lifting the corporate veil]: When a court looks behind a company to hold the people controlling it responsible.
*[paterfamilias]: Latin: "father of the family". The male head of a Roman household, who had full legal capacity.
*[res nullius]: Latin: "a thing of nobody". A thing with no owner, e.g. a wild animal; the first taker becomes owner.
*[res communes]: Latin: things common to all, such as air and the open sea, which no one can own.
*[res publicae]: Latin: public things, such as roads and rivers, held for the public.
*[res privatae]: Latin: private things, owned by individuals.
*[jura in re aliena]: Latin: "rights in another's thing". Rights over property owned by someone else, e.g. lease, easement, mortgage (encumbrances).
*[jura in re propria]: Latin: "rights in one's own thing". Here, ownership of immaterial things like patents and copyright.
*[in rem]: Latin: "against the thing". A right good against the whole world, e.g. ownership.
*[in personam]: Latin: "against the person". A right against one particular person, e.g. a debt.
*[nemo dat quod non habet]: Latin: "no one gives what he does not have". A transferor cannot pass a better title than his own.
*[chattels real]: Leaseholds of land, treated as personal (not real) property in English law.
*[chattels]: Movable goods.
*[choses in action]: Property rights that can only be enforced by legal action, e.g. debts, shares, insurance claims.
*[choses in possession]: Physical movable things that can be held, e.g. a watch.
*[inter vivos]: Latin: "between the living". A transfer made during the owner's lifetime.
*[intestate]: Dying without a valid will.
*[testamentary]: Taking effect by a will, after the maker's death.
*[usufruct]: Right to use and enjoy another's property and its fruits without owning it.
*[servitude]: A right over another's land, such as a right of way (easement).
*[accession]: Gaining ownership of what is added to or produced by one's property, e.g. a calf of one's cow.
*[prescription]: Gaining or losing a right through the passing of time.
*[adverse possession]: Possessing another's property openly as if owner for the period fixed by law, which can lead to ownership.
*[incidents of ownership]: Honoré's list of the separate rights and duties that together make up ownership.
*[bundle of rights]: The idea that property is a set of separate rights (use, sell, lease, exclude) rather than one single right.
*[goodwill]: The value of a business's reputation and customer base.
*[chautari]: A raised stone resting platform under a tree by a trail (चौतारी); public property in Nepal.
*[pauwa]: A traditional public rest house for travellers (पौवा).
*[daijo]: Property a woman receives from her parents' side at marriage (दाइजो).
*[pewa]: A woman's own exclusive property (पेवा).
*[vinculum juris]: Latin: "chain of law". The legal bond that ties a debtor to a creditor in an obligation.
*[actus non facit reum nisi mens sit rea]: Latin maxim: "the act does not make a person guilty unless the mind is also guilty".
*[delict]: Roman and civil law word for a civil wrong; roughly the same as a tort.
*[quasi-contract]: An obligation the law imposes as if there were a contract, to prevent unjust gain.
*[unjust enrichment]: Getting a benefit at another's cost without a legal reason; the law makes you return it.
*[innominate]: "Unnamed". Salmond's leftover class of obligations, such as a trustee's.
*[obligor]: The person who owes an obligation; the debtor.
*[obligee]: The person to whom an obligation is owed; the creditor.
*[ex contractu]: Latin: "from a contract". Roman class of obligations arising from agreement.
*[ex delicto]: Latin: "from a wrong". Roman class of obligations arising from a delict.
*[joint tortfeasors]: Two or more persons who together commit the same tort.
*[exemplary damages]: Extra damages awarded to punish the wrongdoer, not just to cover the loss.
*[vicarious liability]: Being liable for a wrong done by another person, such as an employee.
*[qui facit per alium facit per se]: Latin maxim: "he who acts through another acts himself". Basis of vicarious liability.
*[respondeat superior]: Latin: "let the superior answer". The master is liable for the servant's wrongs at work.
*[strict liability]: Liability without proof of fault, but with some defences allowed.
*[absolute liability]: Liability without fault and without the usual defences.
*[non-natural use]: Special, dangerous use of land that brings in extra risk; needed for the rule in Rylands v Fletcher.
*[act of God]: A natural event, such as an extraordinary flood, that no human care could foresee or prevent.
*[prima facie]: Latin: "at first sight". True unless shown otherwise.
*[Ignorantia juris non excusat]: Latin maxim: "ignorance of the law is no excuse".
*[neighbour principle]: Lord Atkin's test in Donoghue v Stevenson: take reasonable care not to injure people you can foresee being affected.
*[recklessness]: Knowing of a risk of harm and going ahead anyway (हेलचेक्र्याइँ).
*[animus possidendi]: Latin: "intention to possess". The mental element of possession: intention to keep a thing and exclude others.

*[bona fide]: Latin: "in good faith". Honestly, without knowledge of a defect.

*[dharauti]: A cash deposit given to the court as security, for example for release on bail (धरौटी).

*[bigo bharaune]: Recovering from the losing party the amount (बिगो) a court has awarded to the winner (बिगो भराउने).
*[chalan chalaune]: Putting the winning party into actual possession of property under a judgment (चलन चलाउने).
*[likhat pass]: Registering a deed, such as a sale deed, with the land revenue office (लिखत पास).
*[dakhil kharej]: Changing the owner's name in land records after a transfer: the new name is entered and the old one struck off (दाखिल खारेज).
*[namsari]: Moving a record into a new name, such as land records into an heir's name after the owner dies (नामसारी).
*[banda chuttaune]: Separating each person's share when property is partitioned (बण्डा छुट्याउने).
*[lilam]: Auction (लिलाम): public sale of seized property to recover what a judgment orders.
*[Adalati Bandobasta]: "Court management" (अदालती बन्दोबस्त): the chapter of the old Muluki Ain that set out court procedure, cited by rule number, e.g. No. 202. Replaced by the 2074 Codes.
*[Appellate Court]: The court between the District Courts and the Supreme Court until 2073 B.S. (पुनरावेदन अदालत). The Administration of Justice Act 2073 dissolved them; High Courts took their place.
*[Joint Bench]: A bench of two Supreme Court justices (संयुक्त इजलास).
*[Full Bench]: A bench of three Supreme Court justices (पूर्ण इजलास, Supreme Court Rules 2074 r 23(1)). It decides, among others, cases on which a Joint Bench's two justices disagree. Older reports also used the name for larger benches.
*[Division Bench]: A bench of two judges; the name used in older Supreme Court reports (डिभिजन बेञ्च).
*[Special Bench]: A historical Supreme Court bench name used in older reports for important cases, including constitutional challenges (विशेष इजलास). Distinguish it from today's Constitutional Bench.
*[D.N]: Decision number (निर्णय नं.): the serial number NKP gives each Supreme Court judgment it publishes.
*[Larger Full Bench]: A bench of five or more Supreme Court justices (वृहत पूर्ण इजलास, Supreme Court Rules 2074 r 22(1)).
*[writ]: A special order of the Supreme Court or a High Court to protect a right or stop an unlawful act, such as habeas corpus or certiorari (रिट).
*[writ petition]: A petition asking the Supreme Court or a High Court to issue a writ (रिट निवेदन).
*[petitioner]: The person who files a petition, such as a writ petition (निवेदक).
*[extraordinary jurisdiction]: The special power of the Supreme Court and High Courts to issue writs (असाधारण अधिकारक्षेत्र).
*[alternative remedy]: Another legal route, such as an appeal, that the law gives to correct a decision. A writ is usually refused where one exists and works (वैकल्पिक उपचार).
*[jurisdiction]: The legal power of a court or authority to hear and decide a matter (अधिकारक्षेत्र).
*[without jurisdiction]: Done by a court or body that had no legal power to do it.
*[quasi-judicial]: Describes an officer or body that is not a court but decides disputes like one, such as a tax officer (अर्धन्यायिक).
*[quash]: To cancel a decision or order so that it has no legal effect (बदर गर्नु).
*[interim order]: A temporary order that holds things in place until a case or petition is decided (अन्तरिम आदेश).
*[interlocutory order]: An order a court makes during a case, other than its final decision (अन्तर्कालीन आदेश).
*[status quo]: Latin: "the state in which". Things as they are now; an order to keep the status quo stops anyone changing them until the case ends (यथास्थिति).
*[show-cause order]: An order asking the respondent to explain in writing why the order the petitioner seeks should not be made (कारण देखाउ आदेश).
*[burden of proof]: The duty to prove a fact; whoever bears it loses on that point if the fact is not proved (प्रमाणको भार).
*[reverse burden]: A rule requiring the accused to prove a specified fact or defence, shifting that part of the usual prosecution burden.
*[general procedure]: The ordinary court procedure under the general law, now the Muluki Civil and Criminal Procedure Codes 2074 (सामान्य कार्यविधि).
*[summary procedure]: A shorter, faster court procedure for small cases, set by the Summary Procedure Act 2028 (संक्षिप्त कार्यविधि).
*[special procedure]: A procedure that a particular Act sets for the cases under it, in place of the general procedure (विशेष कार्यविधि).
*[special Act]: An Act on one subject that prevails over the general law on that subject (विशेष ऐन).
*[substantive law]: Law that creates rights, duties and offences (सारवान कानून).
*[procedural law]: Law that says how rights are enforced in court: how a case is filed, heard, proved and appealed (कार्यविधि कानून).
*[Special Court]: A court set up by law to hear only certain kinds of cases (विशेष अदालत). Today's Special Court works under the Special Court Act 2059.
*[Zonal Court]: A court of Nepal's old court system, named after a zone (अञ्चल अदालत). It no longer exists.
*[Regional Court]: A court of Nepal's old court system, named after a region (क्षेत्रीय अदालत). It no longer exists.
*[Revenue Tribunal]: A tribunal that hears appeals in tax and other revenue cases (राजस्व न्यायाधिकरण).
*[Interim Constitution]: The Interim Constitution of Nepal 2063 (नेपालको अन्तरिम संविधान, २०६३), replaced by the Constitution of Nepal 2015.
*[Panchayat]: The partyless system of government under the Constitution of Nepal 2019 B.S. (पञ्चायती व्यवस्था).
*[HMG]: His Majesty's Government (श्री ५ को सरकार), the name of Nepal's government under the monarchy.
*[VDC]: Village Development Committee (गाउँ विकास समिति), the village-level local body before the local levels set up under the 2015 Constitution.
*[Nepal Gazette]: The government's official journal, where Acts, rules and notices are published (नेपाल राजपत्र).
*[charge sheet]: The document by which the government lawyer brings a criminal case to court, naming the accused, the offences and the punishment sought (अभियोगपत्र).
*[prison slip]: The court's written order telling the prison to hold a convicted person, and until when (कैदीपुर्जी).
*[detention slip]: The written order given to an arrested person, stating why and under what law he is held (थुनुवा पुर्जी).
*[tahasildar]: The court officer in charge of carrying out judgments, such as recovering amounts and keeping the record of sentences (तहसिलदार).
*[sale deed]: The registered document by which land is sold (राजीनामा).
*[joint property]: Property of an undivided family, in which every coparcener has a share (सगोलको सम्पत्ति).
*[Land Reform Office]: The district government office that decided tenancy and land matters under the Lands Act 2021 (भूमिसुधार कार्यालय).
*[Land Revenue Office]: The government office that registers land deeds and keeps land records (मालपोत कार्यालय).
*[judicial certainty]: The idea that a matter decided by a court should stay settled, not be reopened by one route after another (न्यायिक निश्चितता).
*[retrospective]: Applying to acts done before the law was made.
*[prospective overruling]: Changing a legal rule only for future cases, so that those who relied on the old rule are not harmed.
*[proviso]: A clause, usually starting "provided that", that limits or makes an exception to the rule before it (प्रतिबन्धात्मक वाक्यांश).
*[headnote]: The official summary of a judgment's legal points, printed at its head in NKP.
*[et al]: Latin et alii: "and others". Written after the first-named party when a case has several (समेत).
*[deposition]: A witness's statement given in court (बकपत्र).
*[summons]: A court notice telling a party to appear or answer a case within a time limit.
*[acquitted]: Found not guilty by a court.
*[condone]: To excuse, for example a delay in going to court.
*[Deputy Government Attorney]: A government lawyer who appears for the government and its offices in court (उपन्यायाधिवक्ता).
*[ejusdem generis]: Latin: "of the same kind". General words that follow a list of specific ones cover only things of the same kind.
*[due process]: The idea, from the US Constitution, that life, liberty or property may be taken away only by a fair procedure set by law.
*[ab initio]: Latin: "from the beginning". A decision described as void ab initio has no legal effect from its start.
*[adjective law]: Another name for procedural law, which sets the steps for enforcing rights (कार्यविधि कानून).
*[judicial committee]: The three-member local judicial body under Constitution Article 217 (न्यायिक समिति).
*[court of record]: A court whose proceedings and decisions form an authoritative record; Nepal's Supreme Court has this status under Article 128(2) (अभिलेख अदालत).
*[quorum]: The minimum number of members who must take part for a body to act lawfully (गणपूरक संख्या).
*[constructive res judicata]: The doctrine that a matter which should have been raised in an earlier case may be treated as already settled.
*[intervenor]: A person allowed to join an existing case because it affects their rights or interests.
*[sandhi sarpan]: Neighbourly use or access connected with land, such as a path or water facility (सन्धिसर्पन).
*[arbitration]: A process where the parties give a dispute to a neutral arbitrator for a binding decision (मध्यस्थता).
*[arbitrator]: The neutral person appointed to decide a dispute through arbitration (मध्यस्थ).
*[mediation]: A process where a neutral person helps the parties reach their own settlement (मेलमिलाप).
*[mediator]: A neutral person who helps the parties settle; the mediator does not impose a decision (मेलमिलापकर्ता).
*[award]: The decision made by an arbitrator (मध्यस्थको निर्णय).
*[conciliation]: A settlement process in which a neutral person helps the parties agree and may suggest terms.
*[counterclaim]: A claim the defendant makes against the plaintiff in the statement of defence (प्रतिदाबी).
*[waris]: A person authorised to act for a party in court (वारिस).
*[adhikrit waris]: An authorised attorney whose written authority may include filing, withdrawing or settling a case (अधिकृत वारिस).
*[darpith]: A written endorsement giving the reason a court refuses to register a document (दरपीठ).
*[misil]: The case file containing the court papers and evidence (मिसिल).
*[rai kitab]: The court book recording the decision and its grounds when the hearing ends (राय किताब).
*[banda sawal]: Written questions sent for a witness to be examined through another court (बन्दसवाल).
*[bato ko myad]: Travel time allowed by law in addition to a stated court deadline (बाटोको म्याद).
*[kaid]: Imprisonment imposed as punishment after conviction (कैद).
*[jamanat]: Security given to secure attendance, including property security under the Criminal Procedure Code (जमानत).
*[kompetenz-kompetenz]: The principle that an arbitral tribunal may decide whether it has jurisdiction.
*[ex aequo et bono]: Latin: "according to what is fair and good". Deciding on fairness when the parties expressly authorise it.
*[amiable compositeur]: An arbitrator expressly authorised by the parties to decide on fairness rather than strict legal rules.
*[ad hoc]: Latin: "for this purpose". In arbitration, a process arranged for the particular dispute rather than administered by a permanent institution.
*[sadhak]: Referral of a judgment to a higher court for confirmation where the law requires it (साधक).
*[lagat]: The court record of punishment, money or other relief to be enforced (लगत).
*[jayjat]: Seizing and realising property to recover a judgment debt, fine or other amount (जायजात).
*[pre-emption]: A preferential legal right to take transferred property by paying the required price (हकसफा).
*[tameli]: Depending on context, service of a court notice (तामेली), or placing a proceeding on file without continuing it for the time being (तामेलीमा राख्ने). It does not always mean the underlying right has ended.
*[thunchhek]: The court's decision whether to hold an accused in custody or release them on security or a date to appear (थुनछेक).
*[bigo]: The value or amount in dispute, or the amount a judgment orders recovered; execution provisions may include compensation (बिगो).
*[tahasil]: The court section responsible for carrying out judgments (तहसिल).
*[mudda sakar]: Taking over and continuing a case for a party, such as after that party's death (मुद्दा सकार).
