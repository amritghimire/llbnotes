## Facts

Translated and summarised by us from the Nepali judgment.

**The loan case.** Ram Bhajan Raya Yadav, the plaintiff, sued Pradip Kumar Prasad Shah Kalwar, the defendant, in the Bara District Court. It was a money-lending case (लेनदेन मुद्दा), case No. 1460 (the judgment writes दे.नं. १४६०). Ram Bhajan's written reply in the Supreme Court gives the background. Pradip borrowed Rs 2,51,000 from him to run his household. On 2058/1/10 Pradip signed an unsecured loan deed (कपाली तमसुक) for it. He did not repay the principal and interest within the deed's term. The judgment does not give Pradip's side of the loan.

!!! warning "Unverified"
    We could not confirm from our sources what the abbreviation दे.नं. stands for, so we write "case No.".

**The District Court's judgment.** On 2065/3/1 the District Court held that Ram Bhajan should get the principal and interest he claimed. According to Ram Bhajan, it also fined Pradip in the forgery (किर्ते) part of the case. The judgment does not explain this part. Pradip says the judgment was given in his absence. The judgment does not say whether he had answered the claim.

**The notice of time to appeal.** The District Court issued a notice (myad, म्याद) in Pradip's name, giving him time to appeal (पुनरावेदनको म्याद). The record of service on the notice said this (paragraph 4). Pradip was not found at home, and a member of his household refused to take the notice. So it was pasted on the door of Pradip's house in Amritganj, ward No. 8, Bara, where everyone could see it. Two witnesses were present. One copy was handed to the Village Development Committee (VDC, गा.वि.स.), the local council, to be posted there. The VDC secretary, Jayapat Prasad Yadav, signed. The Supreme Court found that the notice was served on 2065/7/29.

**The lawyer's copy.** According to the District Court's written reply, Pradip's lawyer, Umesh Prasad Jaiswal, took a copy of the judgment on 2065/10/2. He also took a photocopy of the served notice.

**Execution.** Pradip did not appeal. On 2066/2/25 Ram Bhajan applied to have the amount recovered under the judgment (bigo bharaune). Recovering the amount is one part of execution, that is, carrying out a judgment (फैसला कार्यान्वयन). During this execution, a 35-day notice in Pradip's name was pasted on his house door on 2066/6/22 (paragraph 6). Pradip's son and wife applied in the execution for land in their names to be released (फुकुवा). Pradip says they had separated from him by partition on 2061/9/21.

**Pradip's complaint about the notice.** Pradip says that on 2067/3/14 a lawyer took a copy of the judgment for him. The lawyer told him that a notice of time to appeal had already been served on him. Pradip then took a certified copy of the notice. He says it showed that the notice had been overwritten (केरमेट) and served improperly (बेरीतपूर्वक) on 2065/8/29, so that he would not learn of it. He applied to the Bara District Court to cancel the notice and register his appeal.

**Two refusals.** On 2067/4/14 the District Court refused. As the petition reports it, the court gave this reason. There was the 35-day notice, and his lawyer had earlier taken copies of the judgment and the notice. These showed that Pradip knew of the judgment in time, so there was no need to consider the notice. Paragraphs 2 and 7 of the Supreme Court's judgment date this order 2067/4/16.

Pradip then applied to the Appellate Court, Hetauda. That court's written reply says he applied under No. 17 of the Adalati Bandobasta chapter of the Muluki Ain. On 2067/9/1 a joint bench of that court (संयुक्त इजलास) held that the District Court's order was not improper and needed no change. The petition's prayer and the District Court's written reply date this order 2067/6/7.

**In the Supreme Court.** On 2068/2/24 Pradip filed a writ petition in the Supreme Court. He filed it under Articles 32 and 107(2) of the Interim Constitution of Nepal 2063, the constitution then in force. A writ (रिट) is a special order a higher court can make to protect a right. He asked for two writs:

- certiorari (उत्प्रेषण), to quash the District Court's and the Appellate Court's orders; and
- mandamus (परमादेश), ordering his appeal to be registered. The petition does not say whom the order would be addressed to.

He also asked for an interim order (अन्तरिम आदेश): a temporary order to stop the execution until his petition was decided.

The Supreme Court asked each respondent to explain, within 15 days (not counting travel time), why the order he sought should not be issued. On 2068/2/30 it refused an interim order "for now". Its reason was that the petition itself said the judgment was already being carried out.

Three written replies came in. A written reply like this is called a rejoinder (लिखित जवाफ). They came:

- from the Appellate Court, Hetauda (received on 2068/4/8);
- from Ram Bhajan (dated 2068/6/8); and
- from the Bara District Court and its tahasildar (तहसिलदार), the court officer who carries out judgments (dated 2068/6/30).

## Issues

The court framed one question (paragraph 1): can the order the petitioner asked for be issued? Its reasoning answers four narrower questions (our framing):

1. Was the notice of time to appeal served as Adalati Bandobasta No. 110 required?
2. Who must prove that a notice was served improperly, and what must he show?
3. Should a party who let his time to appeal pass get the notice cancelled, and so a fresh chance to appeal?
4. Can a party seek a writ when the law gives another remedy, and the courts have already dealt with his complaint?

## Arguments

The judgment names no counsel who argued in the Supreme Court: the spaces for counsel in its header are blank. The points below come from the writ petition and the three rejoinders, as the judgment summarises them.

**For Pradip, the petitioner (निवेदक):**

- The judgment was given in his absence, and the notice of time to appeal never reached him. He learned of it only on 2067/3/14.
- The notice was overwritten, and the overwriting has no signature of the process server (तामेलदार), the court employee who serves notices. The VDC secretary's signature and the server's signature carry different dates. So it is unclear whether it was served on 2065/7/29 or on 2065/8/29.
- He did not receive the 35-day notice in the execution either, and there is no proof that he did.
- His son and wife had separated from him by partition on 2061/9/21. No. 110 speaks of a person "of the same household" (एकाघरका). So their application in the execution does not show that he got any notice.
- A lawyer took a copy earlier. It is not lawful to presume from this that he knew of the judgment, and so to deny him his right to appeal.
- He relied on Article 13(1) of the Interim Constitution (equality before the law) and on the right to property. He also relied on Article 24(8): "every person shall have the right to be informed of the proceedings taken against him".
- A notice must be served so that it is clearly understood. Under No. 110, he said, the witnesses to a pasting must include one from the ward concerned. He cited the precedent *Khairul Nesa v Appellate Court, Hetauda et al.* (NKP 2058, No. 5/6, p. 331, D.N. 7009). There, the court refused to hold that a defendant had received a 15-day notice of auction (lilam). The witnesses to the pasting were not from the ward concerned, as No. 110 required. Nor were they shown to be the defendant's neighbours.
- Carrying out the judgment while his right to appeal was denied would cause him irreparable harm (अपूरणीय क्षति).

**For the respondents (विपक्षी):**

- **The Bara District Court and its tahasildar.** File No. 534 of 2066 shows a 35-day notice served on Pradip on 2066/6/22. Recovery under the judgment then went ahead. His son's and wife's applications show that he knew of that notice and accepted it. His lawyer took a copy of the judgment and a photocopy of the served notice on 2065/10/2. So his claim that he learned only on 2067/3/14 is neither lawful nor believable. The orders were lawful and did not harm his rights.
- **Ram Bhajan Raya Yadav.** The notice of time to appeal was properly served on 2065/7/29. Pradip did not appeal and accepted the judgment, so it became final. Notices were properly served on him again and again, and his lawyer took a copy on 2065/10/2. Instead of appearing, he made his son and wife file improper complaints in the execution. He filed this petition to harass Ram Bhajan again.
- **The Appellate Court, Hetauda.** Its joint bench decided Pradip's No. 17 application on 2067/9/1 and sent the order to the Bara District Court. That lawful order did not breach Pradip's constitutional or legal rights.

All three asked for the petition to be dismissed.

## Decision

The Joint Bench dismissed the petition. Justice Mishra agreed with Justice Karki's judgment.

1. **The notice was served by the rules** (paragraphs 3 and 4). The court set out No. 110. A court employee who knows the person may hand him the notice wherever he finds him. If the person is not found, the employee goes to his home. There he gives it to an adult of the same household. He does so before a member or secretary of the VDC or municipality and two local gentlemen (भलाद्मी). If none of them is met, or they will not take it, he pastes it on the door where all can see. A copy goes to the VDC or municipality against a receipt, or the back of the served copy is signed. Time runs from the date of pasting. The service record on Pradip's notice followed these steps and showed service on 2065/7/29.
2. **The burden was on Pradip, and he did not meet it** (paragraph 5). Parties have a legal right to learn of a judgment against them and to appeal. No one may be denied the use of a right the law gives. But where a notice was served by No. 110, the petitioner must show credible reasons. He must show which step was missed, and that he did not learn of the notice. Pradip gave no ground or reason. The notice was properly served, so he was not denied a chance to contest the case.
3. **No fresh chance without a reason** (paragraph 5). Suppose a notice was properly served and the person did not contest within its time. The law does not mean that the notice must be cancelled without ground or reason to give him another chance.
4. **A writ is not open after the alternative remedy** (paragraph 6). A court (the judgment does not say which) had checked the notice and treated it as properly served. Recovery had then gone ahead. In the execution, the 35-day notice was pasted on Pradip's door on 2066/6/22, following clause 1 of No. 110. A person harmed under No. 110 may use the alternative remedy (वैकल्पिक उपचार) in No. 208 of the Adalati Bandobasta. Suppose a court has examined an application under No. 208, held that no such defect existed, and acted on that. Then the writ jurisdiction cannot be used to seek a remedy.
5. **Order** (paragraph 7). The District Court's order refusing to cancel the notice, and the Appellate Court's order upholding it, were lawful. The petition was dismissed (खारेज). It was to be struck from the register and its file handed over under the rules.

The judgment does not discuss Pradip's specific complaints: the overwriting, the two dates, his separated wife and son, or the *Khairul Nesa* precedent. Its reasoning rests on the service record. It does not rely on the lawyer's copy of 2065/10/2.

## Ratio decidendi

Our translation of the official headnote, point by point:

1. In fact, the parties to a case hold a legal right to be told of a judgment against them. They also hold a right to appeal against it if they are not satisfied. No one may be denied the use of a right the law gives. (प्रकरण नं. ५)
2. Every person has the right to be told of proceedings taken against him. He also has the right to appeal on points of those proceedings he is not satisfied with. (प्रकरण नं. ५)
3. Suppose a notice in the writ petitioner's name was served following the procedure of No. 110 of the Adalati Bandobasta, the law on serving notices. Then the burden lies on the petitioner himself. He must show sufficient and credible reasons, circumstances and conditions. These must show which step was missed, making the service improper, and that he did not learn of the notice. (प्रकरण नं. ५)
4. Suppose a notice was served by the procedure and form the law lays down, and the person did not contest within its time. The law does not mean that he must keep being given another chance, by cancelling the notice without ground or reason. (प्रकरण नं. ५)

**In short (our summary):** a party has a right to know of a judgment and to appeal. But once a notice is served the way the law requires, the party who says it was improper must prove it. The law does not mean that it must be cancelled without reason to give him a second chance.

The headnote leaves out the court's second ground, on the alternative remedy under No. 208 (paragraph 6). See point 4 under [Decision](#decision).

## Why it matters

- **Proper notice starts the clock.** Parties have a right to learn of a judgment and to appeal. But once a notice is served the way the law says, the time to appeal runs. A bare claim of not knowing will not stop it.
- **The person who says service was improper must prove it** (प्रकरण नं. ५). Here the service record had two witnesses and the VDC secretary's signature. Pradip had to point to the step that was missed, with believable evidence.
- **Pasting on the door is valid service** when the person is not found and his household refuses the notice. It must be done as No. 110 requires: before the required witnesses, with a copy to the VDC.
- **No second chance without a reason.** A court will not cancel a properly served notice just so that a party who let the time pass can contest again.
- **A writ is not a back door** (paragraph 6). The law gave another remedy here, No. 208, and a court had already dealt with the complaint. So the writ jurisdiction could not reopen it. The 2015 Constitution has a related limit. Article 133(2) allows a writ for a legal right only where there is no other remedy, or the remedy is inadequate or ineffective.
- **Execution follows a final judgment** (Unit 8). Once the notice stood, the judgment was final, and recovery of the amount went ahead. Today s 231 of the Code says execution must wait while the time to appeal is running, and while an appeal is pending.
- **Compare [Shivanath Shah Kanu v Bishwanath Shah Kanu](9-10-shivanath-shah-kanu.md) (Unit 9.10).** There, the appeal court decided before the other side had even been sent his notice of time to appeal. So his right to appeal survived. Here, the notice had been served by the rules, so the right ended when the time ran out. In 9.10 the notice had not even been issued; here it had been properly served (our comparison). 9.10 also turned on another rule: when a court gives two judgments, the later one prevails.
- **The present Code has similar but more detailed rules.** Chapter 8 of the Muluki Civil Procedure Code 2074 (ss 99-118) sets out how notices are served and checked. Section 200(3) says the notice of judgment and time to appeal must be served by that chapter. Section 118 adds a remedy for a defendant who lost ex parte because the first notice was improperly served. He may file an appeal that counts as his written defence.

## Full 20-mark answer

??? example "Exam answer (20 marks)"
    **Practice question.** State the facts and examine the ratio decidendi of Pradip Kumar Prasad Shah Kalwar v Ram Bhajan Raya Yadav.

    **Introduction**

    A party who loses a case has a legal right to appeal. But he can use that right only if he knows of the judgment. So when a party is absent at judgment, the court must serve him a notice (myad) of the judgment and of his time to appeal. Under the old Muluki Ain, No. 110 of the Adalati Bandobasta chapter set out how notices were served. Today Chapter 8 of the Muluki Civil Procedure Code 2074 does so.

    What if a party says, after his time to appeal has run out, that he never got the notice? This case answers that question.

    **Citation**

    Pradip Kumar Prasad Shah Kalwar v Ram Bhajan Raya Yadav et al., NKP 2073, No. 10, D.N. 9690 (certiorari). Supreme Court, Joint Bench: Justices Sushila Karki, who wrote the judgment, and Om Prakash Mishra. Decided 2072/12/22.

    **Facts**

    Ram Bhajan sued Pradip in the Bara District Court to recover a loan. On 2065/3/1 the court decided for Ram Bhajan. Pradip says this was in his absence. A notice of time to appeal was issued to Pradip. He was not found at home, and a member of his household refused the notice. So it was pasted on his house door before two witnesses, and the VDC secretary signed. The Supreme Court found that it was served on 2065/7/29.

    Pradip did not appeal. Ram Bhajan applied to recover the amount, and a 35-day execution notice was pasted on Pradip's door on 2066/6/22. According to the District Court, Pradip's lawyer had taken copies of the judgment and the served notice on 2065/10/2.

    Pradip said he learned of the notice only on 2067/3/14. He said it was overwritten, carried two dates, and was served improperly so that he would not know. The District Court refused to cancel it and register his appeal, and the Appellate Court, Hetauda upheld that refusal. Pradip then asked the Supreme Court for certiorari to quash both orders, and mandamus to have his appeal registered.

    **Issues**

    1. Was the notice of time to appeal served as No. 110 required?
    2. Who must prove that a notice was served improperly?
    3. Can a properly served notice be cancelled to give a fresh chance to appeal?
    4. Can a writ be used when another remedy exists and has already been used?

    **Decision**

    The Supreme Court dismissed the petition. The service record showed the steps No. 110 requires. Pradip was not found, his household refused the notice, and it was pasted on the door. Two witnesses were present, and the VDC secretary signed. Pradip gave no ground or reason to show that the service was improper. So he had not been denied a chance to contest the case.

    The court added a second ground. A person harmed by improper service may use the alternative remedy in No. 208 of the Adalati Bandobasta. Once a court has examined such a complaint and rejected it, a writ cannot be used to reopen it. Both lower court orders were lawful.

    **Ratio decidendi**

    The official headnote, drawn from paragraph 5, states four points:

    1. Parties have a legal right to be told of a judgment against them and to appeal against it. No one may be denied a right the law gives.
    2. Every person has the right to be told of proceedings against him and to appeal against them.
    3. Where a notice was served following No. 110, the burden is on the petitioner. He must show credible reasons why the service was improper, and that he did not learn of it.
    4. Someone may not have contested within the time of a properly served notice. The law does not mean that the notice must be cancelled without reason to give him a fresh chance.

    Examining these points, the first two state a right and the last two set its limit. The right is real. Article 20(8) of the Constitution of Nepal 2015 protects one part of it, the right to be informed of proceedings. But the right is met by proper service, not by proof that the party actually read the notice. In effect, the service record is treated as correct until the party shows otherwise. The burden of proof is on him, and he must point to the step that was missed. The fourth point protects finality. A winner should not lose the fruit of his judgment because the loser did not act in time.

    **Comment**

    In my opinion, the ratio strikes a fair balance. Natural justice, especially audi alteram partem ("hear the other side"), requires a fair chance to be heard. Proper service gives that chance. If a bare denial could cancel a notice, no judgment would ever become final.

    But the judgment is short on reasoning. It does not discuss Pradip's specific complaints. These were the overwriting, the two dates, his separated wife and son, and the *Khairul Nesa* precedent he cited. It also gives two dates each for the two lower court orders. Answering those points would have made the burden-of-proof rule more convincing.

    Contrast *Shivanath Shah Kanu v Bishwanath Shah Kanu* (Unit 9.10). There, an appeal court decided one side's appeal before the other side had been sent his notice of time to appeal. So his right to appeal survived. In 9.10 the notice had not even been issued; here it had been properly served. Read together, the two cases suggest that whether a party's time to appeal has properly started is the dividing line.

    **Significance and conclusion**

    The case serves three units. For notice to a defendant (Unit 4A), it shows how a notice is served and who must prove a defect. For appeals (Unit 6), it shows that time to appeal runs from proper service. For execution (Unit 8), it shows that once that time has passed, the judgment can be carried out. It also teaches that a writ cannot reopen a complaint a court has already examined under the alternative remedy.

    The present Code covers the same ground. Section 105 sets the steps for service, including pasting on the door before local witnesses. Section 110 makes the court check every service, and ss 111 and 117 provide for cancelling a bad service and serving again. Section 200(3) requires the notice of judgment to follow Chapter 8, and s 205 starts the thirty days to appeal from its service.

    In conclusion, a party has a right to know of a judgment and to appeal. Where the court serves the notice as the law requires, that right is respected. A party who says otherwise must prove it. And once a court has examined and rejected his complaint under the alternative remedy, he cannot use a writ to reopen it.
