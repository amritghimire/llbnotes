# Procedural Law (Law 302)

**100 marks, pass 35. Compulsory. 3 hours, any 5 of 7 questions.**

Substantive law tells you what your rights and duties are: who owns a house, or what counts as theft. Procedural law tells you how to enforce them. It sets out how a case is filed, how the court hears it, how the losing side can appeal, and how the judgment is carried out.

This paper follows a case through Nepal's courts from start to finish. It starts with the principles that make a procedure fair, such as natural justice, and with which court can hear which case. Then it covers civil and criminal trials, arbitration and mediation, appeals, writs, review and revision, and the execution of judgments. Ten Supreme Court cases are prescribed, and one of them is asked every year.

**Study first:** Unit 2 is asked most. Natural justice was a full question in 2077 and 2080. Limitation, laches, stare decisis and amicus curiae come up as short notes in 2080 and in both old-course papers. Next come jurisdiction (Unit 3, asked in 2077 and 2079) and the case question (every year). After those: revision (Unit 7), execution (Unit 8), and Unit 1 on procedural and substantive law and summary procedure.

Unit 4 covers two Codes, so it has two pages: 4A for civil cases and 4B for criminal cases. The kinds of hearing (syllabus 4.1) are on the criminal page, where the exam asks them.
