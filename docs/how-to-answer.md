# How to write answers

## The time you have

| Paper | Time | Questions | Time per question |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100 marks | 3 hours | 5 × 20 marks | about 35 minutes |
| 50 marks | 1.5 hours | 3 questions | about 30 minutes |

Keep 5 to 10 minutes at the start to choose questions, and 5 at the end to read through.

## A 20-mark long answer

Aim for 3 to 4 written pages. Use this shape:

1. **Introduction (½ page).** Say what the term means in plain words. Give one or two definitions by named jurists, in quotation marks.
2. **Body (2 to 3 pages).** Use headings or numbered points. One idea per point. Use a table if the question asks you to "differentiate".
3. **Nepal's position (½ page).** The Act and section, the Constitution's article, or a Supreme Court case. Examiners look for this.
4. **Critical view (a few lines).** One strength and one weakness, or a criticism by another jurist.
5. **Conclusion (a few lines).** Answer the question asked, in one or two sentences.

Read the verbs in the question:

| Verb | What the examiner wants |
|---|---|
| Define | A precise meaning, ideally with a jurist's definition |
| Explain / Elaborate / Describe | What it is and how it works, in detail |
| Differentiate / Distinguish | A comparison table, point by point |
| Discuss / Examine | Both sides, then your view |
| Critically examine / Assess / Critique | Strengths, weaknesses, and a judgement |
| "In Nepal" / "Nepalese legal provision" | Acts, sections, articles and cases from Nepal |

## A short note

Question 7 asks for 3 short notes, so each is worth about 6 to 7 marks. Spend about 10 minutes on each and aim for one page:

1. Definition in one or two lines, with a jurist if you can.
2. Three to five key points.
3. One example, case or Nepal provision.
4. One closing line.

## A case question

When asked to "state the facts and examine the ratio" of a case, use these headings:

1. **Citation:** names of the parties, NKP year, volume, D.N. (decision number), page.
2. **Facts:** what happened, in 4 to 6 lines.
3. **Issue:** the legal question the court had to answer.
4. **Decision:** what the court ordered.
5. **Ratio decidendi:** the legal principle the decision rests on.
6. **Significance:** why the case matters and which unit it belongs to.
