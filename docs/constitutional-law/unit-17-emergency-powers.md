# Unit 17: Emergency powers

!!! abstract "In one line"
    Article 273 permits exceptional action for a grave emergency, but imposes grounds, parliamentary control, time limits and protections that cannot be suspended.

## Why it matters for the exam

Learn the sequence from declaration to approval, extension and withdrawal. The protected-rights list includes particular clauses, not simply whole subject labels. Emergency does not mean that every right or every court stops operating.

Current disaster legislation, specific compensation procedures and any present emergency proclamation have not been checked. No live emergency is asserted.

| Proclamation | घोषणा | The formal declaration through which the constitutional measure is issued |

| Non-suspendable protection | निलम्बन गर्न नमिल्ने संरक्षण | A right or remedy the emergency provision expressly preserves |
| Bad faith | बदनियत | An improper or dishonest exercise of official authority in the relevant legal setting |
| Compensation | क्षतिपूर्ति | A legally ordered response to established harm under applicable conditions |

## Grounds and territorial scope

Article 273(1) requires a grave emergency affecting sovereignty, territorial integrity or the security of any part of Nepal by reason of war, external aggression, armed rebellion, extreme economic disarray, natural disaster or epidemic. The President may declare or order emergency for the whole country or a particular part. Read the presidential function with Article 66's recommendation framework.

Not every difficult event automatically satisfies this test. A flood may require urgent administration without necessarily establishing every condition for constitutional emergency. The declaration and its legal effects must be identified separately from the factual existence of a disaster.

Under clause (2), a Provincial Government may request the Government of Nepal for an emergency proclamation concerning all or part of the Province where the grave emergency arises from natural disaster or epidemic. The request is not an independent provincial power to declare the Article 273 emergency.

## Parliamentary control and duration

| Clause | Requirement |
|---|---|
| (3) | Lay the proclamation or order before both Houses within one month of issuance |
| (4) | Approval by at least two thirds of the then total membership of both Houses; operation for three months from issuance |
| (5) | Without the required approval, the proclamation or order becomes inoperative |
| (6) | Before the initial period expires, submit a motion for an additional period not exceeding three months |
| (7) | Extension requires the stated two-thirds then-total-membership majority in both Houses |
| (8) | If the House of Representatives is dissolved, the National Assembly exercises the powers in clauses (3), (4), (6) and (7) |
| (13) | The President may withdraw the proclamation or order at any time |

The three-month period in clause (4) runs from **issuance**, not from a later approval date. Extension is a separate constitutional step, not an automatic entitlement. The text provides the additional period in clauses (6)–(7); do not invent indefinite renewal power. Clause (8) transfers the specified parliamentary controls, not all constitutional functions generally.

## Emergency orders

Clause (9) allows necessary orders to meet the emergency, with the force and effect of law while it operates. Such an order is not the same as an Article 114 ordinance or an Article 274 amendment. Emergency authority remains constitutionally allocated; it is not a new unlimited source of power above the Constitution.

## Rights that cannot be suspended

Clause (10) permits suspension within its framework but preserves the following exact protections. Learn the Article or clause as well as the topic. This table paraphrases the list without reproducing every protected provision.

| Protected reference | What to remember |
|---|---|
| 16 | Life with dignity |
| 17(2)(c) and (d) | The specified political-party and union or association freedoms, not all of Article 17 |
| 18 | Equality |
| 19(2) | The specified communication protection, not all of Article 19 |
| 20, 21 and 22 | Justice, crime-victim rights and protection against torture |
| 24 | Protection against untouchability and discrimination |
| 26(1) | The specified religious-freedom clause |
| 29, 30, 31 and 32 | Exploitation, environment, education, language and culture protections |
| 35 | Health |
| 36(1) and (2) | The specified food-right protections |
| 38 and 39 | Women's and children's rights |
| 40(2) and (3) | The specified Dalit protections |
| 41, 42 and 43 | Senior citizens, social justice and social security |
| 45 | Protection against exile |
| 46 in relation to the protected Articles | Constitutional remedy for those protections |
| Habeas corpus | The right to seek this remedy expressly remains available |

**Common error.** Writing all freedoms, all communication rights or all food clauses as protected changes the actual list. Equally, a right not in this list does not become suspended merely because an emergency has been announced. Identify the actual suspension and its constitutional basis.

## Courts, harm and remedies

Clause (11) restricts petitions and questions for enforcement of a right suspended under clause (10). It must be read with clause (10)'s protected remedies. It does not close every court or erase every constitutional protection.

Clause (12) addresses harm from an official act in bad faith during emergency. The victim may petition for compensation within three months after termination of the proclamation or order. The court may order compensation and punish the perpetrator as provided by federal law. The provision does not promise automatic compensation for every emergency-related loss, regardless of bad faith or other legal conditions.

## Related safeguards

Article 85's emergency extension of the House term requires a Federal Act and has its own limits. Article 130(4)'s exception to protection of judicial service conditions concerns emergency due to extreme economic disarray, not every emergency. Extraordinary powers in one field should not be expanded into unrelated powers elsewhere.

The [Rajeev Parajuli judgment](https://nkp.gov.np/full_detail/2166/) addresses constitutional authority and exceptional royal arrangements under the **1990 Constitution**. It illustrates historical disputes about limited power, but is not a current Article 273 judgment.

## Nepal's law

Read all thirteen clauses of Article 273 together with the protected rights, Article 46 and the relevant judicial powers. The constitutional emergency, necessary orders, rights suspension and compensation claim are separate legal questions.

## Compare

| Constitutional emergency | Ordinary urgent administration |
|---|---|
| Requires Article 273 grounds and formal action | Uses the authority available under applicable ordinary law |
| Has special parliamentary and time controls | Follows its own statutory procedures |
| May have specified rights consequences under clause (10) | Urgency alone does not create constitutional suspension |
| Preserves the listed rights and remedies | Constitutional limits continue to govern public action |

## Full 20-mark answers

??? example "Q1. Explain declaration, duration and parliamentary control of emergency under Nepal's Constitution. (20 marks)"
    **Introduction**

    Emergency power permits exceptional action where the constitutional conditions of a grave crisis are met. Its purpose is to address the emergency through law, not to remove all limits on government. Nepal's Article 273 identifies grounds, territorial scope, parliamentary approval, duration, extension and withdrawal. It also regulates necessary orders and protects specified rights and remedies. A complete answer must follow these controls together. The existence of war, disaster or epidemic as a factual event does not by itself establish that every emergency power has arisen. The legal declaration, constitutional conditions and consequences must be separately identified and justified.

    **Grounds for declaration**

    Clause (1) requires a grave emergency affecting sovereignty, territorial integrity or the security of any part of Nepal by reason of war, external aggression, armed rebellion, extreme economic disarray, natural disaster or epidemic. These stated grounds prevent the provision from becoming a general answer to any political difficulty. A declaration may concern the whole country or a specified part. The connection between the event, gravity and constitutional interest matters. An official should not assume that describing a situation as urgent automatically establishes the full constitutional basis for emergency. The particular legal power remains subject to the conditions through which the Constitution makes it available.

    **Presidential and provincial roles**

    Article 273 identifies the President's declaration or order, which should be read with the general presidential framework in Article 66. Clause (2) provides a distinct provincial route in the natural-disaster or epidemic setting. The Provincial Government may request the Government of Nepal for the relevant declaration concerning all or part of its Province. This is not an independent provincial power to create an Article 273 emergency through its own request alone. The distinction preserves the constitutional allocation of authority. A request, a formal declaration and the later approval process are different acts with different legal functions and conditions.

    **Submission to Parliament**

    Clause (3) requires the proclamation or order to be laid before both Houses within one month of issuance. Parliamentary examination is therefore part of the emergency framework rather than a matter left entirely to executive preference. The requirement connects exceptional action with representative control. Its existence should not be mistaken for proof that a particular proclamation has actually been submitted. The relevant record would need to establish compliance. Equally, the power to act initially does not remove the duty to obtain the constitutionally required continuation. An answer must distinguish declaration from the approval that determines its operation through the next constitutional stage.

    **Approval and the initial period**

    Under clause (4), approval requires at least a two-thirds majority of the then total membership of both Houses. The denominator is important. It is not simply a majority of those attending, nor an ordinary legislative vote casually substituted for the special rule. An approved proclamation or order operates for three months from issuance. The period does not begin again on the later approval date. Clause (5) makes the measure inoperative if the required approval is not obtained. These conditions prevent the executive from treating emergency as an indefinitely self-sustaining authority that continues without the representative support specified by the Constitution.

    **Extension**

    Clauses (6) and (7) provide the additional period mechanism. Before the initial period expires, a motion may seek extension for another period not exceeding three months. Adoption requires the stated special majority of the then total membership in both Houses. Extension is therefore a separate legal decision with its own timing and approval conditions. It is not an automatic consequence of the original declaration or an unlimited power to continue emergency for as long as government prefers. The answer should state the additional period actually provided by the text rather than inventing indefinite renewals or assuming that every emergency necessarily lasts the full possible period.

    **A dissolved House of Representatives**

    Clause (8) addresses the situation where the House of Representatives has been dissolved. The National Assembly then exercises the parliamentary powers in clauses (3), (4), (6) and (7). This reflects the importance of continuing constitutional control even when the lower House does not exist. The substitution must be stated precisely. It does not transfer every constitutional function of the entire Parliament to the National Assembly without limit. The particular listed powers concern submission, approval and extension within Article 273. The permanent chamber's role therefore supports continuity of scrutiny while remaining defined by the constitutional provision authorizing it.

    **Necessary orders and rights limits**

    Clause (9) allows necessary emergency orders with the force and effect of law while emergency operates. Such orders are not constitutional amendments and should not be confused with ordinary Article 114 ordinances. Clause (10) separately regulates rights suspension and preserves a detailed list of rights and remedies. This distinction matters because the power to address a crisis does not by itself answer which protection may be suspended. The actual suspension and the protected list must be examined. Emergency action remains constitutionally controlled even where circumstances justify rapid administration, and the Constitution does not describe the declaration as the disappearance of all legal safeguards.

    **Withdrawal, harm and connected provisions**

    Clause (13) permits withdrawal at any time. Clause (12) provides a compensation petition for harm from an official act in bad faith during the emergency, within three months after termination, with consequences under federal law. These provisions give the end of emergency a distinct legal importance. Related rules must also be kept separate. Article 85's extension of the House term requires a Federal Act and has its own limits. Article 130(4)'s service-condition exception concerns extreme economic disarray. Emergency in one field does not automatically authorize every exceptional action that appears elsewhere in the Constitution.

    **Critical view and conclusion**

    Emergency design seeks enough authority to meet a grave crisis without allowing temporary necessity to become permanent uncontrolled government. Its effectiveness depends on genuine examination of grounds, timely parliamentary action and respect for non-suspendable protections. Formal approval alone does not justify conduct outside the particular powers granted. Nepal's framework therefore needs to be explained as a sequence of conditions rather than a single broad grant. Declaration, approval, extension, necessary orders and withdrawal each have a legal role. The strongest answer identifies those roles and shows how constitutional limits continue to control exceptional power throughout the emergency.

    This requires reading the legal order itself. A newspaper description of an emergency cannot establish precisely which rights were suspended or whether the constitutional approval and duration requirements were actually satisfied.
??? example "Q2. Discuss fundamental-rights protection and remedies during emergency in Nepal. (20 marks)"
    **Introduction**

    A state of emergency may create exceptional constitutional consequences, but it does not automatically suspend every right or close every court. Nepal's Article 273 distinguishes declaration, necessary orders and rights suspension, and expressly preserves important protections. Understanding the exact list is essential because some Articles are protected in full while only specified clauses of others are protected. Remedies also remain available in the stated circumstances. A complete answer should therefore reject both the claim that emergency changes nothing and the opposite claim that government is freed from all law. The legal effect depends on the actual constitutional measure and the protection concerned.

    **Suspension is a distinct question**

    Clause (10) permits suspension within its framework, while clauses (1)–(9) address other emergency conditions and powers. A declaration alone should not be treated as proof that every potentially suspendable right has actually been suspended. The particular action and its legal basis need identification. Nor does an ordinary administrative statement create the same effect simply because officials use the language of urgency. This distinction protects legal certainty. People and institutions need to know what measure has been made and which consequences it lawfully creates, rather than infer a complete suspension of constitutional protection from the existence of a serious public problem.

    **Life, equality and justice protections**

    Clause (10) preserves Article 16, Article 18 and Articles 20–22, among others. These concern life with dignity, equality, justice, crime-victim rights and protection against torture. Article 24's protection against untouchability and discrimination is also preserved. Their inclusion shows that exceptional conditions do not eliminate the legal position of persons affected by coercion. The State cannot treat an allegation of danger as sufficient reason to ignore every safeguard. Exact rights still require their own legal analysis, but the emergency provision itself prevents suspension of the specified protections. The protection is constitutional, not dependent solely on an official promise of restraint.

    **Specified freedoms and partial protection**

    The list preserves Article 17(2)(c) and (d), concerning the stated party and union or association freedoms, rather than every freedom in Article 17. It protects Article 19(2), not the whole communication Article, and Article 26(1), not every clause concerning religion. These distinctions are easy to lose in a memorized topic list. Writing freedom or religion without the relevant clause can overstate the provision. Equally, partial listing does not establish that every unlisted clause is necessarily suspended in the actual emergency. The correct answer identifies both the constitutional protection and the specific measure whose effect is being examined.

    **Social, cultural and group protections**

    Article 273(10) also preserves Articles 29–32 and 35, addressing exploitation, environment, education, language and culture and health. It protects Article 36(1) and (2), Articles 38–39, Article 40(2) and (3), Articles 41–43 and Article 45. These references include specified food protections and rights concerning women, children, Dalit, senior citizens, social justice, social security and exile. The list demonstrates that the protected framework is not limited to a narrow account of physical liberty. However, partial references must remain precise. A statement that all of Article 36 or all of Article 40 is listed would alter the constitutional text.

    **Constitutional remedies and habeas corpus**

    Clause (10) preserves the Article 46 remedy in relation to the protected Articles and separately preserves the right to seek habeas corpus. This gives the rights protection an institutional dimension. Habeas corpus tests the legal basis of detention through the appropriate court process, rather than accepting the executive's description of custody as conclusive. Its continued availability should not be confused with a guarantee that every detained person will be released without examination. The court still applies the relevant legal grounds. The important constitutional point is that the opportunity to seek the protected remedy is not removed by emergency suspension.

    **The limit in clause (11)**

    Clause (11) restricts petitions and questions for enforcement of rights suspended under clause (10). It must be read with the same clause's non-suspendable rights and remedies. An interpretation that closes every court would disregard the express preservation of remedies. At the same time, the restriction cannot simply be ignored when a claim seeks enforcement of a right validly suspended within the framework. The legal inquiry therefore needs the actual right and suspension, not a general assumption that every claim is either available or unavailable. Constitutional limits on enforcement should be explained accurately rather than expanded through the emotional force of the word emergency.

    **Bad-faith harm and compensation**

    Clause (12) provides a further protection where an official act in bad faith during emergency causes harm. The victim may petition for compensation within three months from termination of the proclamation or order. The court may order compensation and punish the perpetrator as provided by federal law. The conditions matter. This is not an automatic payment for every loss connected with an emergency, regardless of its cause. Nor should the three-month period be incorrectly counted from the date of the harmful act where the constitutional text uses termination. Exact statutory procedure, amount and consequences require the relevant federal law rather than unsupported figures.

    **Ongoing institutional control**

    Parliamentary approval and time limits also support rights protection. They prevent the proclamation from continuing solely because the executive wishes to retain extraordinary powers. Clause (13) permits withdrawal, and clause (9) ties emergency orders' force to the emergency's operation. Other constitutional rules remain distinct. The existence of emergency does not by itself extend the House term without the separate Article 85 requirements or permit every disadvantageous alteration of judges' conditions. These distinctions show that rights protection depends on both substantive guarantees and controlled institutional authority. A power justified for one purpose cannot automatically be expanded to unrelated constitutional questions.

    **Critical assessment and conclusion**

    Emergency protection requires careful reading of the precise Article and clause references, the actual suspension and the available remedy. Broad slogans create opposite dangers. Assuming ordinary protections are untouched can overlook a lawful exceptional effect, while assuming complete suspension can excuse unconstitutional harm. Nepal's framework expressly preserves a substantial list, related Article 46 protection, habeas corpus and the specified bad-faith compensation route. Its practical value depends on compliance, access and disciplined institutional action. A strong answer explains these safeguards within the controlled emergency process and shows that constitutional government continues even when exceptional measures are legally justified.

## Test yourself

??? question "When does the approved initial three-month period begin?"
    From issuance, not from the later parliamentary approval date.

??? question "Can a Province independently declare the Article 273 emergency through its request?"
    No. Clause (2) provides the specified request to the Government of Nepal.

??? question "Are all Article 17 freedoms listed as non-suspendable?"
    No. The list identifies Article 17(2)(c) and (d).

??? question "Does habeas corpus remain available?"
    The right to seek it is expressly protected by Article 273(10).

??? question "When does the Article 273(12) compensation petition period run?"
    Within three months after termination of the proclamation or order, subject to its bad-faith harm requirement and applicable law.

??? question "Does the National Assembly acquire all parliamentary powers under clause (8)?"
    No. It exercises the specified clause (3), (4), (6) and (7) powers when the House is dissolved.

## One-minute revision

- Establish grave emergency, a stated ground and territorial scope.
- Submission is within one month; approval uses the special majority.
- Initial operation runs three months from issuance.
- Extension is a separate, limited process.
- Protected clauses must be listed precisely.
- Article 46 protection for listed rights and habeas corpus remain.
- Bad-faith harm has a specific post-termination compensation route.
