# Unit 13: Judiciary

!!! abstract "In one line"
    Courts decide legal disputes independently, while constitutional rules govern their appointment, authority, service and accountability.

## Why it matters for the exam

Distinguish the courts, the two recommending councils and the parliamentary hearing. For independence, explain the safeguard and its limit. For jurisdiction, identify the particular constitutional power rather than claiming that the Supreme Court can decide anything.

## 13.1 Independence

Independence protects impartial decisions, especially where government itself is a party. It concerns institutional authority and the judge's ability to decide the particular case. Article 126 allocates judicial powers; Article 128 establishes the Supreme Court's interpretive position. Appointment safeguards, secure service conditions, specified removal and restrictions on incompatible work support independence.

Independence is not immunity from law or a right to decide without reasons. The judiciary must remain within jurisdiction. Fair criticism and lawful accountability must be distinguished from intimidation or obstruction.

## 13.2 Structure

| Institution | Constitutional position |
|---|---|
| Supreme Court | Article 128; court of record and final interpreter of the Constitution and laws |
| High Courts | Article 139 provides a High Court in each Province; Article 144 addresses jurisdiction |
| District Courts | Article 148 provides a District Court in each District; Article 151 addresses jurisdiction |
| Other judicial arrangements | Article 127(2) permits specified local institutions and alternative dispute arrangements under law |
| Specialized courts or tribunals | Article 152 permits institutions for types of cases under federal law, not a tribunal created for one specific case |

The Constitutional Bench is **inside the Supreme Court**, not a separate fourth tier. Under Article 137 it comprises the Chief Justice and four other Justices designated on the Judicial Council's recommendation. Its jurisdiction includes the stated Article 133(1) petitions, listed intergovernmental competence disputes and specified election or disqualification matters. Serious constitutional-interpretation cases may be assigned under Article 137(3).

## 13.3 Supreme Court appointments and qualifications

Article 129 permits a Chief Justice plus a maximum of 20 other Justices. The President appoints the Chief Justice on the Constitutional Council's recommendation and other Justices on the Judicial Council's recommendation.

| Office or route | Qualification stated in Article 129 |
|---|---|
| Chief Justice | At least three years as a Supreme Court Justice |
| High Court route to Supreme Court | Law bachelor's degree and at least five years as High Court Chief Judge or Judge |
| Practice route | Law bachelor's degree and at least fifteen years' continuous practice as senior advocate or advocate |
| Distinguished-jurist route | At least fifteen years' continuous work in the judicial or legal field with the stated distinction |
| Judicial-service route | At least twelve years in a Gazetted first-class or higher judicial-service post |

Article 129(5) requires Nepalese citizenship and states the alternative routes. Do not combine their distinct requirements into a single invented qualification. The Chief Justice's six-year term remains subject to vacancy provisions, including retirement at 65 under Article 131.

## 13.4 Councils and parliamentary hearing

Article 153's Judicial Council includes the Chief Justice as chair, the federal Law and Justice Minister, the senior-most Supreme Court Justice, a jurist appointed on the Prime Minister's recommendation and a senior advocate or advocate with at least twenty years' experience appointed on the Nepal Bar Association's recommendation. The last two members have four-year terms under its clause (2).

Article 284's Constitutional Council has the Prime Minister as chair, the Chief Justice, House Speaker, National Assembly Chairperson, House opposition leader and Deputy Speaker. For a Chief Justice recommendation the Law and Justice Minister is included under clause (2). Do not confuse its appointment role with the Judicial Council's separate function.

Article 292 provides parliamentary hearings and a fifteen-member joint committee under federal law. Recommendation, hearing and formal appointment are different stages. The hearing provision does not change which council recommends under Article 129.

## 13.5 Service conditions and privileges

Article 130 leaves remuneration and conditions to federal law while protecting against disadvantageous alteration. Its exception concerns an emergency declared because of extreme economic disarray. The Article also contains specific pension and disqualification rules; it does not guarantee every benefit after impeachment or the stated criminal conviction.

Article 132 restricts other offices and contains a specified judicial-inquiry or legal-research exception. Article 135 restricts post-retirement legal practice, mediation and arbitration proceedings before offices or courts. These provisions address conflicts and incentives. They are not a general personal exemption from ordinary law.

## 13.6 Removal and vacancy

Article 131 distinguishes resignation, retirement at 65, impeachment, the stated illness-based removal through the appropriate recommendation, conviction for an offence involving moral turpitude and death.

Article 101(2) allows one fourth of the then total House of Representatives membership to move impeachment against the Chief Justice or Supreme Court Justice on its specified grounds. Adoption requires at least two thirds of that House's then total membership. Article 101 provides inquiry arrangements, non-performance of duties after proceedings commence, a reasonable opportunity to defend and stated consequences. This differs from the both-Houses adoption rule for President or Vice-President under Article 101(1).

## 13.7 Supreme Court jurisdiction

| Provision | Authority | Qualification |
|---|---|---|
| 128(2), (4) | Final interpretation and compliance with interpretations and legal principles | Read the actual holding, not every party's argument |
| 133(1) | Review of laws on the stated inconsistency grounds | The Constitution specifies standing, grounds and effect |
| 133(2)–(3) | Extraordinary protection of rights and public-interest legal questions, including writs | Observe the categories and legislative-proceedings proviso |
| 133(4)–(6) | Original, appellate, confirmation, revision and review functions under law | Not an unlimited power detached from procedure |
| 134 | Transfer in specified circumstances | Apply its constitutional conditions |
| 137 | Constitutional Bench matters | A bench of the Supreme Court, with stated jurisdiction |

Article 133(1) permits invalidation from the beginning or from the decision date as specified. When reviewing its own judgment under Article 133(4), Justices other than those who gave the earlier judgment conduct the review. Do not confuse review, revision and appeal; see [Procedural Law](../procedural-law/unit-07-writ-review-revision.md).

## 13.8 Contempt

Article 128(4) authorizes contempt proceedings and punishment according to law for obstruction of justice or disregard of protected orders or judgments. High Court and District Court contempt powers appear in Articles 139(2) and 151. Constitutional authority does not itself supply every statutory penalty or procedural detail.

Explain the purpose as protecting administration of justice. Do not treat any disagreement with a judgment as automatically punishable, or invent a general exemption for all speech. The legal elements and applicable procedure need the relevant law and case authority.

## Nepal's law

Read Articles 101, 126–156, 284 and 292. Separate the institution's power, appointment process, service safeguards and accountability. Details left to federal law must be checked before stating exact statutory rules.

## Full 20-mark answers

??? example "Q1. Explain judicial independence and the safeguards provided by Nepal's Constitution. (20 marks)"
    **Introduction**

    Judicial independence means the ability of courts and judges to decide legal questions without improper interference. It matters particularly when government, a powerful institution or a politically influential person is involved in a dispute. Independence is not a privilege granted for the private benefit of judges. Its purpose is impartial justice for those who need the court's protection. Nepal's Constitution supports this purpose through judicial authority, appointment arrangements, service protection, specified removal and restrictions on incompatible work. The safeguards must be read together with accountability and jurisdiction, because independence does not authorize a judge to disregard law or assume every function of government.

    **Constitutional judicial authority**

    Article 126 provides for the exercise of powers relating to justice through courts and other judicial institutions according to the Constitution, laws and recognized principles of justice. It also requires compliance with court orders and decisions made in the course of hearing cases. Article 128 establishes the Supreme Court as a court of record and final interpreter of the Constitution and laws. These provisions give judicial decisions a legal position distinct from executive preference. If the authority accused of acting unlawfully could conclusively dictate the result of its own case, constitutional protection would depend on that authority's goodwill rather than independent legal examination.

    **Institutional structure**

    Article 127 identifies the Supreme Court, High Courts and District Courts, while other provisions allow specified judicial arrangements under law. Article 139 provides a High Court in each Province and Article 148 a District Court in each District. Article 152 allows specialized institutions for types of cases but prohibits creation for a particular specific case. This distinction protects adjudication against a tribunal specially designed for one dispute. The Constitutional Bench under Article 137 is part of the Supreme Court, not an independent fourth tier. Clear structure helps identify the competent institution and prevents a claim that any body may assume judicial authority without a constitutional or legal basis.

    **Appointment arrangements**

    Article 129 distinguishes appointment of the Chief Justice from other Supreme Court Justices. The President appoints the Chief Justice on the Constitutional Council's recommendation and other Justices on the Judicial Council's recommendation. Different institutions therefore participate in the process rather than one political official possessing every stage personally. Article 292 also provides the parliamentary hearing framework. These arrangements should not be treated as a guarantee that improper influence is impossible. Their constitutional purpose is better understood through transparent qualification, institutional participation and responsibility. The recommendation, hearing and formal appointment remain different acts with different legal sources.

    **Qualifications and professional competence**

    Independence requires competence as well as freedom from pressure. Article 129 requires at least three years as a Supreme Court Justice for the Chief Justice. For other Justices it states alternative routes through High Court service, legal practice, distinguished legal or judicial work and judicial service. The routes have their own durations and requirements. They should not be combined into one invented qualification. Constitutional eligibility supplies a legal boundary to appointment, while responsible selection must also examine the candidate within that framework. An independent institution cannot perform its role effectively if legal competence is treated as irrelevant to the authority to decide disputes.

    **Service conditions**

    Article 130 regulates important service protections. Remuneration and conditions are generally left to federal law, but cannot be altered to a judge's disadvantage except within the stated emergency-related qualification. The exception concerns extreme economic disarray, not every occasion on which the executive wishes to reduce judicial security. Financial protection matters because an authority should not be able to punish an inconvenient decision through selective disadvantage. The Article also contains pension conditions and exclusions. These should be read accurately rather than described as an unconditional entitlement to benefits after every form of departure or conviction.

    **Tenure and removal**

    Security of office protects a judge from ordinary political displeasure, but does not remove accountability. Article 131 identifies vacancy circumstances including resignation, retirement at 65, impeachment, the specified illness-based process, the stated criminal conviction and death. Article 101(2) provides impeachment grounds and a demanding House of Representatives process for the Chief Justice or Supreme Court Justice. Adoption requires at least two thirds of that House's then total membership. The process includes a reasonable opportunity to defend. The purpose is a legally controlled removal mechanism, not a claim that every unpopular decision proves misconduct or that judges can never lawfully be removed.

    **Restrictions addressing conflicts**

    Article 132 limits engagement in other offices, while allowing its specified judicial-inquiry or legal-investigation and research arrangement. Article 135 restricts post-retirement legal practice and mediation or arbitration proceedings before offices or courts. These provisions help address conflicts and incentives that might affect adjudication. Their exact scope matters. A safeguard should not be expanded into an unsupported claim about every form of work or personal activity. The relevant question is how an outside position could compromise the judicial role and what the Constitution actually prohibits or permits. Independence is strengthened by following the legal restriction rather than inventing a broader one.

    **Jurisdiction and accountability**

    Independent courts must act within jurisdiction and provide reasoned legal decisions. Article 133 identifies different Supreme Court powers and their conditions. Article 137 assigns particular matters to the Constitutional Bench. These provisions do not create a general judicial administration of every policy question. A court may need to decide legal authority in a politically important dispute, but the existence of political importance does not either eliminate review or make every political choice judicial. Legal accountability also requires distinguishing the court's holding from a party's argument and applying the relevant procedure for appeal, revision or review instead of treating all reconsideration as the same power.

    **Protection of the administration of justice**

    Contempt powers protect justice against obstruction and disregard of protected orders and judgments. Article 128(4), together with the relevant law, provides the Supreme Court's authority. High Court and District Court powers have their own provisions. Such protection should not be confused with freedom from all criticism. The legal elements and procedure must be established before consequences are asserted. Compliance likewise matters because independent adjudication loses practical value if lawful orders can simply be ignored. Institutional respect is therefore owed to the legal administration of justice, rather than to an unlimited personal claim that judges' views may never be questioned.

    **Conclusion**

    Nepal's constitutional safeguards combine judicial authority, professional qualifications, structured appointment, service security, controlled removal and restrictions on conflicting roles. Their purpose is impartial adjudication within a superior legal framework. Practical independence still requires responsible institutional conduct and respect for lawful decisions. An answer should therefore avoid both treating judges as subordinate executive officers and treating independence as personal immunity. The judiciary protects people and constitutional boundaries when it can decide without improper pressure, explain its reasoning and remain accountable through the lawful procedures that govern its own authority.

??? example "Q2. Explain the Supreme Court's composition, appointment, jurisdiction and accountability. (20 marks)"
    **Introduction**

    The Supreme Court occupies a central position in Nepal's constitutional order as a court of record and final interpreter of the Constitution and laws. Its authority is substantial but defined. Composition, appointment and jurisdiction must therefore be examined alongside service safeguards and accountability. Articles 128–137 provide the main framework, with the councils, hearing and impeachment provisions supplying important related rules. A complete answer should distinguish these mechanisms rather than assume that being the highest court permits every kind of decision. The Constitution authorizes independent adjudication and constitutional protection while also establishing the boundaries and responsibilities of the institution exercising them.

    **Composition and the Chief Justice**

    Article 129 provides for a Chief Justice and a maximum of twenty other Justices. A person must have served as a Supreme Court Justice for at least three years to qualify as Chief Justice. The office has a six-year term, subject to constitutional vacancy provisions including retirement at 65. These requirements concern different aspects of office. A stated term should not be described as a guarantee of service beyond the constitutional retirement age. Article 129 also provides an acting arrangement through the senior-most Justice in the specified circumstances. Continuity is therefore addressed through law rather than informal assumption of the Chief Justice's functions.

    **Appointment and qualifications**

    The President formally appoints the Chief Justice on recommendation of the Constitutional Council and other Supreme Court Justices on recommendation of the Judicial Council. Article 129(5) sets alternative qualification routes for other Justices. They include the stated High Court service, continuous legal practice, distinguished work in the legal or judicial field and qualifying judicial service, with the respective periods and requirements. Article 292 provides parliamentary hearings. Recommendation, hearing and appointment are separate processes. This distinction prevents an answer from assigning every role to one council or treating a hearing as authority to disregard the constitutional qualifications of the proposed appointee.

    **The two councils**

    Article 153's Judicial Council is chaired by the Chief Justice and includes the specified judicial, ministerial and appointed professional members. It performs constitutionally identified recommendation and advisory functions concerning judges and justice administration. Article 284's Constitutional Council is chaired by the Prime Minister and includes the stated constitutional office holders. Its recommendation role includes the Chief Justice and specified constitutional officials. The Law and Justice Minister is included for a Chief Justice recommendation under clause (2). Their different composition and responsibilities matter. Similar names do not make the councils interchangeable, and the general hearing framework does not erase the particular allocation made by Article 129.

    **Interpretation and binding legal principles**

    Article 128 gives the Supreme Court final authority to interpret the Constitution and laws. Clause (4) requires compliance with its interpretations and legal principles laid down in deciding cases. This makes judgments a major source for understanding constitutional rules. Reading them requires care. The text may contain facts, allegations, submissions, reasoning and separate views. A party's quoted argument is not automatically the court's legal principle. Historical decisions also apply the constitutional instrument then in force. Proper use of authority therefore identifies the actual holding and legal setting rather than treating every sentence printed on a court website as the same kind of binding proposition.

    **Review of laws**

    Article 133(1) provides extraordinary review of laws on the stated grounds of inconsistency. It includes constitutional inconsistency and specified conflicts involving federal, provincial or local legislation. The provision permits the relevant declaration from the beginning or from the decision date. These consequences should not be assumed to arise through one universal formula. The particular provision and judgment determine the legal effect. Article 1's supremacy supplies the broader framework, while Article 133 identifies the court's role in determining invalidity. A law's political popularity or long use does not itself answer a properly raised question of constitutional authority.

    **Extraordinary protection and writs**

    Article 133(2) addresses fundamental rights, certain other legal rights and constitutional or legal public-interest disputes. Clause (3) includes habeas corpus, mandamus, certiorari, prohibition and quo warranto. Each remedy has a distinct purpose and must be appropriate to the dispute. The clause also limits interference with internal legislative and privilege proceedings except on absence of jurisdiction. The Supreme Court therefore cannot be described either as powerless whenever a matter is politically sensitive or as entitled to supervise every internal parliamentary irregularity. Jurisdictional analysis identifies the legal question and the constitutional basis for the particular form of intervention sought.

    **Other jurisdiction and the Constitutional Bench**

    Article 133(4)–(6) recognizes further original, appellate, confirmation, revision and review powers according to law. Review of the Court's own judgment is conducted by Justices other than those who decided the earlier judgment. Article 137 establishes the Constitutional Bench within the Supreme Court, comprising the Chief Justice and four other Justices designated on recommendation of the Judicial Council. Its specified matters include the relevant Article 133(1) petitions, intergovernmental competence disputes and election or disqualification disputes. A serious constitutional-interpretation case may be assigned under clause (3). It is a special bench of the Court, not a separate constitutional court outside it.

    **Service, removal and responsibility**

    Articles 130–132 address service conditions, vacancy and incompatible offices, while Article 135 restricts specified post-retirement practice. Article 131 recognizes resignation, age retirement and other defined circumstances rather than removal at ordinary executive pleasure. Impeachment under Article 101(2) has specified grounds and requires the stated House of Representatives majority. These safeguards support independence while preserving accountability. They do not justify a judge ignoring legal limits. Article 136 also assigns the Chief Justice responsibility for effective administration of justice. Institutional independence concerns lawful decision making and administration, not the removal of every constitutional duty from those who exercise judicial power.

    **Contempt and conclusion**

    Contempt authority under Article 128(4) protects justice against obstruction and disregard of protected orders or judgments, with procedure and punishment according to law. It is not a substitute for establishing the legal elements or a reason to invent penalties from memory. The Supreme Court's position is best understood through this combination of authority and restraint. It interprets the superior framework, reviews legality and protects rights within defined jurisdiction, while appointment, service and removal rules govern its own institution. A strong answer explains these connections and distinguishes the Court's independent legal role from unrestricted control over every public dispute.

## Test yourself

??? question "How many Supreme Court Justices can the Constitution provide besides the Chief Justice?"
    A maximum of twenty under Article 129(1).

??? question "Which council recommends the Chief Justice and which recommends other Justices?"
    Constitutional Council for the Chief Justice; Judicial Council for other Supreme Court Justices.

??? question "Is the Constitutional Bench a separate court tier?"
    No. It is a bench within the Supreme Court under Article 137.

??? question "Can the Chief Justice's six-year term override retirement at 65?"
    No. Read the term with Article 131's vacancy rules.

??? question "Which House adopts impeachment of a Supreme Court Justice?"
    The House of Representatives under Article 101(2), by at least two thirds of its then total membership.

??? question "Does constitutional contempt authority establish every penalty?"
    No. Applicable law and procedure must be checked before asserting a penalty or consequence.

## One-minute revision

- Independence protects impartial adjudication within law.
- Supreme, High and District Courts form the named court structure.
- Recommendation, hearing and appointment are separate stages.
- Judicial and Constitutional Councils have different roles.
- Service security and controlled removal support independence.
- Supreme Court powers differ under Articles 128, 133 and 137.
- Contempt protects justice and remains subject to legal conditions.
