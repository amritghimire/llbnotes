# Unit 8: Salient features of the Constitution of Nepal 2015

!!! abstract "In one line"
    The Constitution combines popular sovereignty, a federal parliamentary republic, rights, inclusion and institutions that control public power.

## Why it matters for the exam

A feature answer needs explanation and constitutional evidence. Avoid a bare list of labels. State what each feature changes for an institution or a person, then identify an implementation challenge without inventing performance statistics.

## Constitutional supremacy and popular sovereignty

Article 1 makes the Constitution fundamental law and inconsistent law void to the extent of inconsistency. Article 2 vests sovereignty and state power in the people. Read these together: popular sovereignty is exercised through constitutional arrangements, not through an individual official's unlimited claim to speak for everyone.

## Federal democratic republican state

Article 4 describes the state's constitutional character. Article 56 gives the Federation, Provinces and Local Level a constitutional place. Articles 57–60 and Schedules 5–9 organize powers and financial relations. Federalism is more than additional administrative offices; it is a constitutional distribution of responsibility.

Republicanism distinguishes the head of state from hereditary monarchy. Article 62 provides presidential election through an electoral college. It does not convert Nepal into a presidential executive system.

## Parliamentary government and pluralism

Article 74 expressly adopts a pluralist parliamentary form. Article 76 regulates government formation and Article 100 regulates confidence. The Prime Minister heads the Council of Ministers; the President is the constitutional head of state under Article 61. Parliamentary responsibility is a control on executive authority, not a general power to dissolve the House whenever disagreement occurs.

## Rights, duties and remedies

Part 3 protects fundamental rights and includes duties of citizens. The rights cover personal liberty, equality and participation as well as social concerns such as education, health, food and housing. Read the exact beneficiary, conditions and restrictions of each Article rather than declaring every guarantee absolute.

Article 46 provides constitutional remedies, with important jurisdiction under Articles 133 and 144. Article 48 states citizens' duties. A duty to obey the Constitution does not give an official permission to ignore a person's rights.

## Inclusion and social justice

The Preamble and provisions including Articles 18, 38 and 42 address equality and inclusion. Articles 84 and 86 structure federal representation, including constitutionally specified participation requirements. Political inclusion is not limited to declaring that all people may vote. The design also addresses representation and exclusion.

Special measures must be explained through the relevant provision. Inclusion is neither permission for arbitrary favour nor a claim that every distinction between people is prohibited.

## Secular and socialism-oriented character

Article 4 describes Nepal as secular and socialism-oriented. Its explanation of secular includes religious and cultural freedom, including protection of religion and culture handed down from time immemorial. Read it with religious freedom under Article 26, which contains its own conditions and prohibitions. Do not replace the constitutional wording with an unrestricted general definition imported from another jurisdiction.

The socialism-oriented description must be read with the Preamble's democratic commitments, fundamental rights and Article 25's property framework. It is not a general authorization to ignore property rights or abolish political competition.

## Independent judiciary and constitutional oversight

Articles 126–128 organize judicial authority and interpretation. Articles 133 and 137 provide important review and Constitutional Bench functions. Constitutional institutions also perform specified oversight and protective roles. Their authority comes from their own provisions, not from an unlimited general permission to supervise every branch.

Judicial independence protects legal decision making. It does not remove jurisdictional limits or make every policy question a judicial task.

## Directive principles, policies and obligations

Part 4 directs governmental purposes and policy. Article 55 restricts court questions concerning implementation of matters in that Part. Distinguish those provisions from enforceable rights in Part 3. A policy's enforcement limit does not justify ignoring a separately applicable fundamental right.

## Amendment and controlled extraordinary power

Article 274 provides constitutional amendment procedures and limits, including provincial involvement for specified subjects. Article 273 provides emergency powers within conditions and safeguards. Neither provision is a general exception to constitutional government. Emergency is a regulated constitutional arrangement, not the disappearance of law.

## Full 20-mark answers

??? example "Q1. Discuss the salient features of the Constitution of Nepal 2015. (20 marks)"
    **Introduction**

    The Constitution of Nepal 2015 establishes a framework for popular authority, a federal parliamentary republic and protection of rights. Its salient features should be explained as connected legal choices rather than memorized as unrelated labels. Constitutional supremacy limits ordinary authorities, federalism distributes responsibility and parliamentary government connects the political executive with representation. Rights, inclusion and remedies concern the position of people affected by public power. The particular meaning of each feature comes from the constitutional text. An assessment must therefore identify provisions and explain their effects without assuming that the existence of a guarantee proves its complete practical realization.

    **Supremacy and popular sovereignty**

    Article 1 declares the Constitution fundamental law and makes inconsistent law void to the extent of inconsistency. Article 2 vests sovereignty and state power in the Nepali people and requires constitutional exercise of that power. These provisions work together. The people supply the source of authority, while the Constitution structures its lawful exercise. A public official cannot claim unlimited power merely by asserting popular support. Similarly, an ordinary parliamentary majority cannot ignore a constitutional limit simply because it can enact legislation. Supremacy provides the legal standard by which the exercise of authority can be examined.

    **Federal and republican structure**

    Article 4 identifies Nepal's constitutional state character, while Article 56 establishes the Federation, Provinces and Local Level. Article 57 and Schedules 5–9 allocate powers, and Articles 59–60 address financial arrangements. These provisions make territorial government part of the constitutional design rather than merely an administrative convenience. Republicanism concerns the head of state. Article 62 provides for presidential election through an electoral college, replacing hereditary monarchical headship in the present framework. Federalism and republicanism answer different questions, so neither should be used as a substitute for explaining the other. Both remain subject to a common superior Constitution.

    **Parliamentary democracy and pluralism**

    Article 74 expressly adopts a pluralist parliamentary form of governance. Article 76 regulates appointment of the Prime Minister and formation of the Council of Ministers. Article 100 provides confidence and no-confidence procedures. This arrangement distinguishes the head of state from the political head of government. The existence of an elected President therefore does not make Nepal a presidential executive system. Parliamentary responsibility is intended to make political authority answerable through representative institutions. However, its actual operation requires scrutiny rather than automatic acceptance of every executive preference. Constitutional procedures remain binding even where the executive has strong party support.

    **Rights, duties and remedies**

    Part 3 recognizes a broad range of fundamental rights and includes duties of citizens. The rights concern liberty, equality, participation and social needs, including education, health, food and housing. Each provision has its own beneficiaries, conditions and limitations. Article 46 provides constitutional remedies, while Articles 133 and 144 contain important judicial jurisdiction. These arrangements connect constitutional recognition with avenues for protection. Article 48 states duties, but those duties do not authorize officials to disregard rights. A feature answer should therefore show both sides of the relationship. People have constitutional responsibilities, and public institutions remain responsible for acting within the constitutional rights framework.

    **Inclusion and social justice**

    The Preamble and provisions including Articles 18, 38 and 42 address equality and the correction of exclusion. Federal representation under Articles 84 and 86 includes specified participation arrangements. These features recognize that formal permission to participate may be inadequate where historical or social barriers persist. Inclusion nevertheless needs a legal basis and accurate explanation. It is not a general permission to favour chosen supporters. Nor does equality prohibit all constitutionally authorized protective measures. The constitutional question is how the particular provision addresses disadvantage while preserving a reasoned and lawful framework for public decision making.

    **Secular and socialism-oriented character**

    Article 4 describes the state as secular and socialism-oriented. Its explanation of secular must be read on its own terms, including religious and cultural freedom and protection of religion and culture handed down from time immemorial. Article 26 supplies the particular religious-freedom framework and its restrictions. The socialism-oriented description must likewise be read with democratic competition, fundamental rights and the property provisions of Article 25. It does not permit an official to disregard the rest of the Constitution. These features express constitutional direction within a broader legal arrangement, rather than unrestricted powers that cancel the rights and institutional limits surrounding them.

    **Independent judiciary and oversight institutions**

    Articles 126–128 establish important judicial authority and the Supreme Court's interpretive role. Article 133 provides review and remedies, while Article 137 establishes the Constitutional Bench and its jurisdiction. These provisions help resolve disputes about legal limits and territorial competence. Other constitutional institutions perform their own specified oversight and protective functions. Institutional independence is important because constitutional restraints need decision makers capable of examining misuse. However, independence does not mean unlimited power. Each body must act within its assigned authority, and judicial review should not be confused with unrestricted judicial control of every policy or administrative choice.

    **State policies and constitutional safeguards**

    Part 4 contains directive principles, policies and obligations. They guide state purposes through a different structure from every individual fundamental-rights claim. Article 55 limits judicial questioning concerning implementation of matters in that Part. This distinction should be explained rather than presenting every policy aspiration as directly enforceable in the same way. Article 274 provides constitutional amendment procedures and limits, including provincial involvement for specified subjects. Article 273 regulates emergency powers. Both show that constitutional change and extraordinary action remain legal processes with conditions. Neither is a general permission to suspend the constitutional order at an office holder's convenience.

    A concrete way to connect these features is to consider a person challenging an unlawful public decision. Popular authority explains why the institution exists, the allocation of powers determines whether it may act and the rights framework limits what it may do. An independent court can examine the properly raised legal claim, while public and parliamentary scrutiny address responsibility. The features therefore matter together. Their value is not merely that they make the document detailed, but that they organize a lawful relationship between institutions and the people they govern.

    **Critical assessment and conclusion**

    The Constitution combines representative government with legal restraints and commitments to social inclusion. Its main practical challenge is converting institutional design and rights into accountable action. Clear responsibility, accessible remedies, lawful implementing legislation and compliance matter alongside the text. No invented statistics are needed to explain these requirements. The Constitution's salient features form a connected system in which popular power is structured, divided and limited for constitutional purposes. A strong answer therefore explains what each feature does, identifies its provision and recognizes the difference between a constitutional commitment and evidence that its purpose has already been fully achieved.

??? example "Q2. Explain how the Constitution combines democracy, rights, inclusion and limited government. (20 marks)"
    **Introduction**

    Democracy, rights, inclusion and limited government are related constitutional concerns. Democracy asks how public authority is connected to the people. Rights protect the position of persons affected by that authority. Inclusion addresses barriers that can prevent equal participation, while limited government requires institutions to remain within lawful powers. The Constitution of Nepal 2015 combines these concerns rather than allowing one to cancel all the others. The result must be explained through actual provisions. A claim of electoral victory cannot alone justify every public act, just as a general appeal to a constitutional value cannot replace the text's particular conditions and procedures.

    **Popular authority within the Constitution**

    Article 2 vests sovereignty and state power in the people and requires their exercise according to the Constitution. Article 1 makes that Constitution fundamental law. Popular authority therefore does not mean that whichever institution currently commands support may act without restraint. The Constitution identifies the lawful ways in which institutions represent and serve the people. This connection protects democracy over time. If a temporary majority could remove the conditions of free participation whenever convenient, later public choice could become ineffective. Constitutional limits preserve the framework through which democratic authority can continue to be meaningfully exercised.

    **Representative and responsible government**

    Article 74 adopts pluralist parliamentary government. Article 76 regulates government formation, while Article 100 addresses confidence. These arrangements connect the political executive with a representative chamber and establish methods of responsibility. Parliamentary government is not merely a convenient way to appoint a leader. It also provides an institutional setting in which executive conduct can be examined and political support tested according to law. The President's separate head-of-state role under Articles 61 and 66 must be distinguished from the Prime Minister's leadership of the Council of Ministers. Clear institutional roles make responsibility easier to identify and constitutional limits easier to explain.

    **Rights protect democratic conditions**

    Fundamental rights do more than protect individuals after a political decision. Freedom, equality and participation help create conditions in which democratic decisions can be made openly. Article 17 addresses important freedoms and Article 18 equality. These provisions have their own terms and limitations, so their effect cannot be explained through an unrestricted slogan. Their relationship with democracy is nevertheless clear. People need legal protection if they are to question officials, express disagreement and take part in public life. Rights therefore support democratic government rather than standing outside it as an inconvenience that elected institutions may ignore.

    **Inclusion and meaningful participation**

    Formal equality may not remove barriers created by longstanding exclusion. The Constitution's Preamble and provisions including Articles 18, 38 and 42 address this concern. Representation under Articles 84 and 86 also includes constitutionally specified participation arrangements. Inclusion thus concerns both legal protection and access to institutions. Its purpose should not be reduced to an assumption that every person already participates on equal terms. At the same time, a protective measure needs to fit its constitutional authority. Inclusion is not arbitrary favour. A sound answer explains the connection between the recognized disadvantage, the legal provision and the measure's intended contribution to participation.

    **Social conditions and constitutional direction**

    Rights concerning education, health, food and housing recognize that the use of freedom depends partly on practical conditions. Part 4 also directs state purposes through principles, policies and obligations. These commitments need careful legal distinction. Article 55 restricts judicial questions concerning implementation of matters in Part 4, while Part 3 includes separate rights and remedy provisions. It would be wrong either to promise the same direct remedy for every policy or to dismiss all social commitments as meaningless. Their constitutional position directs attention to legislation, institutional action and accountability as well as the particular judicial claims available under the text.

    **Limits through territorial and institutional division**

    Federalism distributes governing responsibility among the Federation, Provinces and Local Level under Articles 56–60 and the constitutional lists. This can provide more opportunities for participation and reduce dependence on a single centre. Functional division also matters. The legislature, executive and courts exercise different constitutional roles. Parliamentary connections do not erase judicial authority under Articles 126–128 or review under Article 133. These arrangements help prevent the concentration of all public power in one institution. Yet divided authority requires cooperation and clear responsibility, because a person seeking protection should not face a system in which every institution blames another.

    **Remedies and independent examination**

    Article 46 provides a right to constitutional remedies. Articles 133 and 144 establish important Supreme Court and High Court jurisdiction. Independent legal examination allows claims to be assessed even where political channels are ineffective for an individual. Article 126(2)'s compliance requirement is also important, because a favourable decision must be capable of producing protection. Judicial authority has its own limits. Courts interpret and apply law within jurisdiction rather than governing every policy question. This combination of independence and legal discipline supports limited government by making official power reviewable without treating the judiciary as an unrestricted substitute for representative institutions.

    **Lawful change and extraordinary powers**

    Limited government does not mean that constitutional arrangements can never change or that emergencies cannot be addressed. Article 274 provides amendment procedures and substantive limits, with provincial involvement for specified proposals. Article 273 provides an emergency framework under conditions and safeguards. These provisions illustrate constitutional control of exceptional action. Neither urgency nor a large majority automatically removes the need to follow the relevant procedure. The ability to adapt is therefore combined with protection against convenience-driven disregard of the framework. An answer should state these connections rather than assuming that democracy necessarily requires complete constitutional flexibility or complete resistance to reform.

    These connections also explain why implementation must be evaluated carefully. A failure in access to a remedy may weaken rights protection without proving that the right has no legal existence. A failure of scrutiny may weaken parliamentary responsibility without changing the constitutional classification of the system. Identifying the particular duty and institutional failure makes criticism useful. It directs attention to the corrective action required rather than allowing a general statement about constitutional success or failure to replace examination of the legal arrangement.

    **Critical assessment and conclusion**

    The constitutional design brings together public choice, rights, inclusion and restraints because each can support the others. Their practical success requires accessible institutions, responsible political conduct and implementation of lawful duties. A written guarantee alone does not prove that exclusion, arbitrary action or barriers to justice have ended. Equally, practical difficulty does not erase the value of the legal standard against which conduct can be assessed. Nepal's Constitution seeks a democratic order in which authority remains accountable and people can participate with legal protection. Understanding that connection gives a stronger explanation than treating democracy and constitutional limits as opposing principles.

## One-minute revision

- Supreme law and popular sovereignty work together.
- Nepal is federal, democratic, republican and parliamentary.
- Rights, duties and remedies have distinct legal roles.
- Inclusion addresses participation and disadvantage.
- Read secular and socialism-oriented through the Constitution's own provisions.
- Courts, oversight, amendment and emergency rules operate within limits.
- Textual commitment and practical implementation are different questions.
