# Constitutional Law (Law 304)

**100 marks, pass 35. Compulsory. 17 units and 10 prescribed cases.**

A constitution is the top law of a country. It sets up the state's main bodies: the legislature that makes laws, the executive that runs the government, and the judiciary that decides disputes. It also sets limits on what those bodies can do, mainly through people's fundamental rights.

This paper has two halves. Units 1 to 6 cover the theory every constitution rests on: what a constitution is, how constitutions are classified, constitutionalism, the rule of law, separation of powers and federalism. Units 7 to 17 apply that theory to Nepal. They trace the constitutions from 1948 to 2007, then work through the Constitution of Nepal 2015 (2072 B.S.): its main features, rights, directive principles, the federal legislature, executive and judiciary, interpretation, political parties, amendment and emergency powers. Ten Supreme Court cases are prescribed.
