# Unit 6: Legality of object and consideration

!!! abstract "In one line"
    Parties cannot require an unlawful act merely by agreeing to it. The Code also distinguishes an impossible bargain from a valid bargain later made impossible by a fundamental change.

!!! warning "Unverified"
    Nepal Civil Code provisions were checked against the Nepali government text through Jestha 2076 and Law Commission repository text. Complete later-amendment currency remains unverified. The wagering analysis is expressly a statutory inference, not a verified civil wagering precedent.

## 6.1 Meaning and importance of lawful object and consideration

The object is the aim of the bargain. Consideration is the exchange in Indian and English doctrine. Nepal's section 505(1)(घ) requires lawful obligation; it does not separately list consideration. A clear price for an unlawful act does not cure the unlawful object. Example: agreeing to pay someone to carry prohibited goods remains a legality problem even if both sides consent freely.

## 6.2 Unlawful agreements

Sections 501 and 517 bar relevant unlawful obligations. Section 517(2)(ङ) concerns agreements contrary to law or involving prohibited matters, and (ट) unlawful purpose. Identify the actual prohibition and apply it. Do not declare an unpopular activity illegal without a source. A void agreement is not automatically a criminal offence. Criminal liability requires the relevant offence and proof.

## 6.3 Agreements opposed to public policy

Section 517(2)(च) addresses immoral purpose, public order and public interest. Other listed grounds protect public facilities, lawful marriage and access to courts. Public policy is not the examiner's or judge's personal dislike. Explain the protected interest and statutory ground.

Example: an arrangement purporting to stop someone using a public facility raises 517(2)(ग). A clause blocking enforcement of legal rights through courts raises (घ). Do not infer that every arbitration agreement is invalid. Lawful arbitration has a separate statutory framework and is not identical to destroying all legal enforcement rights.

## 6.4 Restraint of trade and exceptions

Section 517(2)(क) generally invalidates restraints on a profession, trade or business not prohibited by law. Its proviso covers four stated situations:

1. Sale of business goodwill with specified time and place restrictions on the seller's similar business.
2. Restrictions between partners concerning competing activities while partnership continues.
3. Restrictions between former partners for a reasonable period or specified place after separation, in the statutory terms.
4. Restrictions on accepting service with a competitor during service or for a specified period after retirement from the relevant service.

Read the actual conditions. The employment exception's wording is not a guarantee that every unlimited restriction is valid. A shop purchase restricting its seller's competition in the specified setting differs from a promise never to work anywhere in Nepal. Do not replace Nepal's listed exceptions with the English general reasonableness formulation or India's differently framed section 27.

## 6.5 Void agreement

Section 517(2) lists restraint of lawful trade, lawful marriage, public facilities and court enforcement; unlawful, immoral or public-interest-opposed arrangements; uncertainty, initial impossibility or fictitious bargains, vagueness, incapacity, unlawful purpose and essential mutual factual mistake. Subsection (3) treats the void arrangement as invalid from its beginning. Subsection (4) preserves a remaining lawful part where it is enforceable. Do not assume that every illegal fragment always destroys every distinct obligation, or that a prohibited transaction can always be saved by deleting a word.

Compare [Unit 5](unit-05-free-consent.md). A voidable contract has a different status. Section 519 separately addresses unenforceability through form or authority defects.

## 6.6 Supervening impossibility

Section 502 distinguishes initial from later impossibility. Section 531 deals specifically with a fundamental change making contractual performance impossible. Its examples include later illegality, uncontrollable events preventing performance, destruction or unavailability of the necessary subject and inability to perform a personal service through death or the stated incapacity.

Section 531(3) excludes mere difficulty, reduced profit or loss, failure of a relied-on third party, strike or lockout, additional taxes and failure of one purpose in a multi-purpose contract from being treated as fundamental change under that subsection. Do not say that every strike or cost increase automatically discharges the contract. Under (4), negotiations to review terms may occur in the stated circumstances. Under (5), money already paid, future payment and accounting for completed work or reasonable expenses must be addressed. Under (6), parties may agree to continue after the specified uncontrollable-event situation ends.

Example: destruction of the unique hired item differs from a supplier finding the agreed price less profitable. Prove actual prevention, not merely the existence of a flood somewhere in the country.

## 6.7 Wagering agreement

The checked section 517 has no separately named wagering category comparable to India's Contract Act section 30. That does not make Nepal betting lawful. Criminal Code 125(1) prohibits gambling and organising it; (4) prohibits betting and organising it, and (5) describes stakes on a side's loss or win in a legally playable game or process. The explanation to (2) contains specified authorised-game and lottery exceptions.

Reading that prohibition with Civil Code 505(1)(घ), 517(2)(ङ) and (ट), **an agreement requiring prohibited betting is unlawful and void**. This is an inference from the statutory provisions. It is not a claim that every conditional payment, insurance contract or authorised game has the same status.

!!! warning "Unverified"
    No current Nepal civil judgment directly deciding recovery of wagering stakes or all collateral loans was verified. Do not promise recovery or nonrecovery on every related transaction. The scope of an authorised exception needs its own current-law check.

DN 7774, [Nepal Government v Bhairaja Pradhan](https://nkp.gov.np/full_detail/2561), applies the Gambling Act 2020, not Criminal Code 125. Paragraphs 45–46 distinguish whether money-staked marriage cards can be gambling from proof that the defendants actually played for money. Keep the historical label.

## Full 20-mark answers

??? example "Q1. Explain unlawful objects, void agreements and restraint of trade in Nepal."
    !!! warning "Unverified"
        This answer uses the checked Nepal text through Jestha 2076. Later amendments remain incompletely verified.

    Contract law recognises voluntary commitments only within legal limits. Parties cannot make prohibited conduct enforceable by fixing a price, signing a document or declaring that they consent freely. Nepal's Civil Code section 505 requires a lawful obligation, while sections 501 and 517 address unlawful performance and invalid agreements. These provisions protect interests beyond the immediate wishes of the parties. The law also distinguishes different reasons for invalidity and recognises limited exceptions to particular prohibitions. An answer must therefore identify the relevant ground and conditions rather than announce that every objectionable or unequal bargain is automatically illegal.

    **Object, exchange and legal control**

    The object of an agreement is what the parties seek to achieve through it. Consideration, in the Indian and English textbook sense, concerns what is exchanged for the promise. A person might agree to pay money for a delivery, but the legality question depends on what is to be delivered and under which law. Payment for prohibited conduct does not make that conduct lawful. Nepal's section 505 does not separately list consideration among its four conditions. The student should explain the comparative concept without replacing the Code's requirement of lawful obligation with a foreign statutory formula.

    Section 517(2)(ङ) addresses agreements contrary to law or involving matters prohibited by prevailing law. Clause (ट) concerns unlawful purpose. The first practical step is to identify the actual legal restriction. An activity is not illegal merely because the writer disapproves of it or another person finds it inconvenient. Conversely, the parties' private approval cannot remove an established prohibition. This approach ties the conclusion to a legal source. It also prevents confusion between a civil consequence of invalidity and criminal liability, which requires a separate offence and proof of its particular elements.

    **Public order and protected interests**

    Section 517(2)(च) concerns immoral purposes, public order and public interest. Other clauses protect matters such as public facilities, lawful marriage and the ability to enforce legal rights through courts. These grounds show that agreements can affect people beyond their signatories. For example, a private arrangement cannot necessarily deprive the public of a facility being enjoyed as such. The statutory ground must be connected with the particular facts. Public policy is not an unrestricted power to discard any agreement considered unattractive, and an examination answer should explain the interest being protected rather than use the phrase as a conclusion without reasons.

    The court-enforcement restriction also requires careful interpretation. A clause attempting to prevent a person from enforcing a legal right is not automatically identical to an agreement to use a lawful arbitration process. Arbitration has its own statutory framework and legal safeguards. A student should not invalidate every arbitration clause by reading one phrase in isolation. The example demonstrates a wider method: identify the actual effect of the term, read relevant provisions together and avoid an interpretation that destroys a recognised legal process without examining its statutory basis. Legal categories must be applied to substance rather than a label alone.

    **Void agreements and their effect**

    Section 517 lists several grounds of invalidity beyond unlawful purpose. They include certain restraints, uncertainty, initial impossibility or fictitious agreements, vagueness, incapacity and both parties' mistake about an essential fact at formation. These grounds are not all forms of crime. A bargain too vague to define an obligation may be void without either party committing an offence. Likewise, a shared essential mistake can defeat validity even where both acted honestly. Distinguishing the grounds helps explain why invalidity is a legal category broader than intentional wrongdoing and why the same moral description cannot answer every problem.

    Subsection (3) treats a void contract as invalid from the beginning. This differs from a voidable contract under section 518, which the injured party may have a court set aside and which remains operative until avoidance under the checked text. Section 519 separately addresses unenforceability through specified form or authority defects. The distinction affects how a claim is explained and which statutory route applies. The answer should name the relevant consequence and avoid using void, voidable, illegal and unenforceable as interchangeable expressions. Questions about benefits already transferred may also require separate legal analysis rather than an automatic assumption about recovery.

    **Restraint of trade and exceptions**

    Section 517(2)(क) generally invalidates a contract restraining a person from a profession, trade or business not prohibited by law. This protects lawful economic activity. Its proviso identifies four settings which are not treated as prohibited restraints in the statutory terms. The first concerns sale of business goodwill and restrictions on the seller's similar business for the specified period and place. The commercial reason is understandable: a buyer paying for established customer attraction may need protection from the seller immediately taking that attraction back. The actual terms must still fit the statutory setting.

    The second setting concerns partners and competing activity during partnership. The third concerns restrictions between former partners for a reasonable period or specified place after separation. The fourth concerns accepting competing service during the relevant employment or for a specified period after retirement from it. These must be stated as Nepal's own listed exceptions. They do not justify a blanket statement that every promise never to work anywhere is enforceable. Identify the relationship, activity, period and place where relevant. A supposed exception cannot be assumed merely because a printed contract uses the words competition or confidentiality.

    **Separation and comparative caution**

    Section 517(4) permits enforcement of a remaining lawful part despite an invalid part. This requires asking whether a workable lawful obligation remains. It does not guarantee that any prohibited scheme can be repaired by deleting an inconvenient word. The bargain's structure matters. An independent lawful supply term may differ from an indivisible promise whose whole purpose is unlawful. The student should identify the relevant part, explain the proposed separation and consider whether the remainder can operate according to law. This is more precise than assuming either that every defect destroys everything or that every defect can always be isolated.

    India has its own Contract Act sections 23, 24 and 27, while English restraint doctrine follows a different legal framework. Their language and exceptions must not be substituted for Nepal's section 517. In conclusion, the lawfulness requirement balances private autonomy with protected public and individual interests. A strong answer identifies the actual prohibition, distinguishes invalidity categories, explains the listed trade-restraint exceptions and considers lawful separation. It uses examples to show why freely agreed terms are not necessarily legally enforceable, while avoiding the equally mistaken idea that courts may reject any bargain merely because its outcome seems commercially unwise.

??? example "Q2. Explain initial and supervening impossibility under the Civil Code."
    !!! warning "Unverified"
        This answer uses the checked Nepal text through Jestha 2076. Later amendments remain incompletely verified.

    Contractual performance may be impossible when an agreement is made, or a later event may prevent performance of an initially possible commitment. These situations should not be treated as identical. Nepal's Civil Code section 502 distinguishes them, section 517 addresses initial impossibility among void-contract grounds and section 531 provides a detailed rule for fundamental changes making performance impossible. The subject balances reliable performance against the fact that some obligations cannot lawfully or physically be fulfilled after a qualifying change. It also prevents a disappointed party from escaping merely because performance has become expensive, difficult or less profitable than expected.

    **Initial impossibility**

    Initial impossibility exists when the relevant obligation cannot be fulfilled at its creation. Section 502(1) says such an obligation need not be performed. Section 517(2)(ज) addresses contracts impossible at formation or fictitious contracts within the void-contract framework. Suppose parties bargain for the use of a unique identified item which has already been destroyed. The factual premise may make the promised performance impossible from the start. Depending on what each party knew, essential mutual mistake may also require examination. The answer should identify the actual obstacle rather than assume that every failed expectation amounts to an impossible obligation.

    The legal consequence differs from ordinary breach. If the agreement is void for the relevant initial defect, the problem is not simply that one party later decided not to fulfil a valid promise. Nevertheless, the status of the agreement does not automatically decide every issue about money or property already transferred. Separate statutory rules may need consideration. Section 502(3) also addresses obligations partly capable of performance. This reminds the student to examine the scope of the impossibility. A problem affecting one portion does not necessarily justify assuming that every other part of the obligation must have the same legal treatment.

    **Later impossibility and fundamental change**

    Section 502(2) directs that consequences of subsequent impossibility are determined according to law. For contracts, section 531 is the main detailed provision discussed here. It concerns a fundamental change from the circumstances of contracting which makes performance impossible. The requirement connects the event with actual inability to perform. An earthquake or flood elsewhere in the country is not enough merely because it is serious. The answer must show how the event prevents the promised performance. This connection distinguishes the legal ground from a general appeal to sympathy after a disruptive event has occurred.

    Section 531(2) identifies situations including later illegality, uncontrollable events preventing performance, destruction or loss of availability of a necessary subject, and inability to deliver a personal service through death or the stated incapacity. These examples explain the kind of change contemplated. If the promised personal service depends on a particular person's skill, substitution by any stranger may not be the same performance. Likewise, loss of an essential unique subject differs from ordinary inconvenience in buying an available replacement. The statutory text and contract must be read together to identify what performance was actually required.

    **Circumstances which do not suffice**

    Subsection (3) is especially important because it lists circumstances not treated as fundamental change for this purpose. Mere difficulty does not suffice. Reduced profit or financial loss does not suffice merely as such. The provision also identifies failure or incapacity of a relied-on third party, strike or lockout, additional taxes and failure of one purpose where the contract served several purposes. These details should be stated accurately, rather than replaced by a broad slogan that any event outside the debtor's control automatically releases performance. The Code distinguishes ordinary contractual risks from the stated qualifying changes.

    For example, a supplier agreeing to a fixed price may later discover that materials cost more. That fact alone is different from destruction of the only identified subject of performance. Similarly, an increased tax may make the bargain unattractive without satisfying the statutory condition. The law would lose much of its ability to support planning if every adverse market movement automatically discharged promises. At the same time, the parties may have terms addressing a particular risk. A good answer examines the applicable agreement and statute instead of assuming that the descriptive label force majeure settles every legal question.

    **Negotiation and accounting consequences**

    Under section 531(4), parties may negotiate review or variation in the circumstances stated there, unless the contract provides otherwise. Negotiation is different from a unilateral power to stop performing. It gives the parties a way to address changed practical circumstances without automatically declaring the original commitment legally impossible. The distinction is useful in an examination problem involving cost or delay. The answer can explain why the statutory discharge claim fails while recognising that a negotiated adjustment may remain possible. Commercial cooperation and automatic legal release are separate mechanisms with different requirements and effects.

    Where the qualifying impossibility exists, subsection (5) addresses financial consequences. It deals with returning money already paid, the status of future or outstanding payment and accounting for work performed or money paid before the change, including reasonable expenses in its terms. The parties should not be left with an unexplained statement that the contract ends and nothing else matters. A factual answer identifies what was paid, what work was done and what remains outstanding. It then applies the statutory accounting rules rather than promising a universal refund or automatic compensation regardless of the actual circumstances of the transaction.

    **Continuation, comparison and conclusion**

    Subsection (6) permits agreement to continue performance after the specified uncontrollable-event situation ends. This shows that a disruptive event does not always require the parties to abandon their practical relationship permanently. They may decide how to proceed within the law. The student's account should therefore distinguish the event, its legal consequences and any later agreement to continue. It should also avoid assuming that every delay has become permanent impossibility. The precise contractual obligation and the nature of the change matter when assessing whether section 531 applies and what further arrangement the parties can make.

    India's section 56 and English frustration doctrine offer comparisons, but neither supplies Nepal's detailed statutory list or accounting rules. In conclusion, initial impossibility concerns a defect present when the obligation arose, while supervening impossibility concerns a later fundamental change preventing performance. The correct method identifies the obligation, proves the change and its effect, checks the exclusions and explains the consequences for completed work and payments. That method protects both the reliability of bargains and fairness where the law recognises a genuine impossibility, without turning every expensive or disappointing contract into an opportunity for unilateral escape from agreed obligations.

## Test yourself

??? question "Does every void agreement involve a crime?"
    No. Uncertainty or mutual essential mistake may invalidate without establishing an offence.

??? question "Can every noncompetition term be justified as an employment exception?"
    No. Check section 517's actual setting, terms and other law.

??? question "Does a rise in material prices automatically discharge performance?"
    No. Difficulty or reduced profitability alone is excluded by 531(3).

??? question "Is a flood's existence enough to invoke 531?"
    No. Establish the required connection with impossible performance.

??? question "Does Nepal's lack of a separate wagering heading legalise betting?"
    No. Read the Civil Code with Criminal Code 125.

??? question "Does English law still invalidate a contract merely because it relates to gambling?"
    Gambling Act 2005 section 335 removes that fact alone as a bar, while preserving other unlawfulness rules.

## One-minute revision

- Lawful consent cannot validate an unlawful object.
- Use a real prohibition, not personal disapproval.
- Section 517 distinguishes several void grounds and permits lawful separation.
- Learn all four restraint-of-trade exception settings.
- Initial impossibility differs from later fundamental change.
- Section 531 excludes difficulty and reduced profit alone.
- Nepal betting analysis needs Criminal Code 125, with its qualifications.
