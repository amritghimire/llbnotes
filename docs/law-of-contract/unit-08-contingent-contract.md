# Unit 8: Contingent contract

!!! abstract "In one line"
    A contingent contract makes the relevant obligation depend on a future event. An agreement may exist before the event, while the duty to perform is still conditional.

!!! warning "Unverified"

## Why it matters for the exam

- Contingent contract appears as a short-note topic in 2078 Q7(b).
- Learn the positive-event, negative-event and fixed-time rules with a timeline.
- A condition is not automatically a wager or a right to ignore every other contractual duty.

## Key terms

| Term | Nepali | Plain meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Contingent contract | सांयोगिक करार | A contract whose relevant obligation depends on a future event |
| Positive contingency | घटना घट्ने शर्त | A condition requiring an event to happen |
| Negative contingency | घटना नघट्ने शर्त | A condition requiring an event not to happen |
| Conditional obligation | शर्तयुक्त दायित्व | A duty that operates only under the stipulated condition |
| Fixed-time contingency | निश्चित समयको शर्त | A condition tied to an event within a defined period |

## 8.1 Meaning and features

Section 513 addresses an agreement to do or not do something depending on a future event. For example, A agrees to supply equipment if a specified permit is obtained. The permit event must be distinguished from the promised supply itself. Identify what the parties agreed, the event, who must perform and when the condition is satisfied.

Features include an identifiable agreement, a future conditional event, and an obligation whose operation depends on that event. The remaining capacity, consent, certainty, lawfulness and form requirements still apply. Do not read section 513 as validating an unlawful or meaningless condition.

A contingent obligation differs from a public reward accepted through doing the advertised act under 511. It also differs from an unconditional obligation which later becomes impossible under 531. A payment date is not necessarily a contingency merely because it lies in the future. Read what the agreement makes conditional.

## 8.2 Rules of contingent contracts

| Section 513 | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| (1) | Where performance depends on an event happening, the relevant obligation does not arise until it happens | Supply conditional on a specified permit |
| (2) | A condition involving a person's future act cannot produce the obligation if that person acts so that performance of the specified act becomes impossible | A named person takes an action making the stipulated personal act impossible |
| (3) | Where performance depends on an uncertain event not happening, the obligation arises when that event becomes impossible | Payment if a unique item cannot return, after its destruction makes return impossible |
| (4) | For an event required within fixed time, impossibility of its happening within the required time brings the stated invalidity rule into operation | A permit required by the stated deadline cannot be obtained within it |
| (5) | Where an event must not happen within fixed time, the obligation arises when it does not happen within that time, or when it becomes certain it will not happen within that time | A promised payment if a specified consignment does not arrive by the deadline |

Distinguish **not yet happened** from **cannot happen**. A late truck on Monday might still arrive before Friday. Its being late on Monday alone does not prove Friday's negative condition. Also distinguish absence of the condition from breach of an independent promise. If A separately promised to apply for the permit, failure to apply may raise another issue. Do not erase that independent promise simply because equipment delivery was conditional.

An event impossible from the outset raises sections 502 and 517, rather than a valid obligation waiting forever. A contingent contract is not automatically a wager: the underlying purpose may be a genuine lawful transaction. [Unit 6](unit-06-legality.md) explains why betting requires its separate legality analysis.

## Nepal's law

Section 513 is the main provision. Read it with 504–505 on valid formation, 515 on interpretation, 517 on initial invalidity and 531 on later impossibility. Avoid claiming a sixth subsection of 513: the checked text has five.

## Full 20-mark answers

??? example "Q1. Explain the meaning, features and rules of contingent contracts in Nepal."
    !!! warning "Unverified"
        This answer uses the checked Nepal text through Jestha 2076. Complete later-amendment currency remains unverified.

    **Meaning and function**

    A contingent contract makes the relevant obligation depend on a future event. People use such arrangements when they wish to commit to a transaction but cannot yet know whether a necessary event will occur. Nepal's Civil Code section 513 addresses these contracts. A simple example is an agreement to supply specified equipment if a stated permit is obtained. The parties may already have reached an agreement, although the conditional supply obligation does not arise until the stipulated event. This distinction between agreement and the operation of an obligation is central to understanding the subject and avoiding a mistaken allegation of premature breach.

    A condition does not remove the ordinary requirements of validity. The parties must still satisfy the applicable rules on binding consent, capacity, certainty, lawful obligation and form. An agreement to perform prohibited conduct if permission from a private friend is obtained does not become lawful merely because it is conditional. Likewise, a condition so vague that the obligation cannot be identified raises a certainty problem. Section 513 therefore operates within the wider Code. It supplies rules for event-dependent duties, rather than a separate method of avoiding every other safeguard that normally controls a legally enforceable transaction.

    **Features and identification of the condition**

    The answer should identify the agreement, the future event, the person who must perform and the connection between event and duty. The event is not simply any future fact mentioned in the document. A future payment date may fix when an unconditional debt must be paid without creating the same kind of contingent obligation. A genuine condition, by contrast, makes the relevant duty depend on whether something happens or does not happen. Reading the words and purpose of the agreement is therefore necessary before assigning the label. Section 515's interpretation framework supports attention to the parties' common intention and the agreement as a whole.

    A contingent arrangement also differs from acceptance of a public reward. Under section 511, performing the advertised task can supply the acceptance contemplated by the offer. In a contingent contract, the analysis concerns an agreed obligation dependent on the stated event. The distinction can affect both formation and performance. It also differs from a contract initially unconditional but later rendered impossible by a fundamental change under section 531. Classification should follow the legal structure and timeline. The same event, such as a permit refusal, can raise different questions depending on what the parties originally agreed and what duties were unconditional.

    **A positive future event**

    Section 513(1) provides that an obligation dependent on an event happening does not arise until that event happens. If supply is expressly conditional on obtaining a particular permit, the buyer should not treat the equipment as overdue merely because the permit remains undecided before the relevant time. The condition must first be examined. Proof may include the agreement and the actual permit decision. The fact that one party expected approval is not equivalent to approval itself. An expectation about a future event cannot automatically be substituted for the event the parties chose to make legally significant.

    Subsection (2) addresses an event involving a person's future conduct. If that person acts so that the stipulated act becomes impossible, the obligation does not arise in the stated circumstances. This is more than ordinary uncertainty about whether the person will eventually choose to act. The particular conduct must make the specified future act impossible in the relevant setting. The provision discourages treating every theoretical possibility as an obligation waiting indefinitely. It also requires care in identifying the act contemplated by the parties, rather than changing the condition after the event to suit whichever outcome one side now prefers.

    **A negative future event**

    Section 513(3) addresses an agreement dependent on an uncertain event not happening. The obligation arises when that event becomes impossible. The distinction between delay and impossibility matters. If a unique item has not returned today, it might still return tomorrow. If it is destroyed in circumstances making the stipulated return impossible, the negative condition can be established on a different basis. A student should therefore explain why the event can no longer happen instead of treating every temporary absence as final non-occurrence. The legal rule requires a demonstrated change in the possibility of the event, not merely impatience with its timing.

    Parties can reduce uncertainty by specifying a time window. Section 513(4) deals with an event required to happen within fixed time and the stated consequences when it becomes impossible within that framework. Subsection (5) deals with non-occurrence within fixed time. The obligation arises where the event does not occur within the period or it becomes certain that it will not occur within that period. These rules should be applied with the actual deadline. A condition about arrival by Friday is not necessarily satisfied by absence on Monday. Conversely, certainty of non-arrival within the window may matter before the last minute expires.

    **Impossibility, legality and other duties**

    A condition impossible from the beginning must also be considered under sections 502 and 517. The answer should not describe such an arrangement as necessarily valid but waiting forever. Equally, a contingent payment is not automatically a wager. A genuine equipment transaction may depend on a permit without being an agreement to stake money on a game. Legality requires its own inquiry, including the relevant civil and criminal provisions where betting is actually involved. The mere presence of an uncertain event does not determine every legal consequence or replace examination of the purpose and substance of the agreement.

    The condition must also be separated from independent duties. A party may promise to make a proper application for the permit while the later supply remains conditional on approval. Failure to obtain approval and failure to apply are not automatically the same issue. In conclusion, contingent contracts allow parties to organise future uncertainty through legally defined conditions. A strong analysis identifies the event, distinguishes happening from non-happening, applies the fixed-time rules and examines impossibility without losing sight of other commitments. Nepal's five subsections provide the framework, while the ordinary validity rules and the agreement's meaning remain essential to a justified legal result.

    **Why the distinctions matter**

    An obligation depending on an event happening is not the same as one depending on that event failing to happen. A deadline can further change the analysis, and a later obstacle to an unconditional obligation raises another legal question. Nepal's Civil Code section 513 regulates several contingent situations, while section 531 concerns fundamental changes making performance impossible. These distinctions matter because a person may otherwise demand performance too early or wrongly treat an existing obligation as discharged. The correct approach begins with the actual words of the agreement and a timeline identifying formation, the event, any deadline and the point at which performance is demanded.

    Consider equipment which A agrees to deliver to B if a specified permit is obtained. Now compare a promise to pay if a specified consignment does not arrive by Friday. Both arrangements mention future uncertainty, but their triggering events differ. The first looks for a positive event, approval. The second looks for a negative event within a defined period, non-arrival. Neither can be analysed merely by saying that something is uncertain. The legal conclusion depends on which event the parties selected and how that event controls the relevant obligation. Their other promises must also be read rather than assumed away.

    **Positive contingency**

    Under section 513(1), an obligation dependent on an event happening does not arise until that event happens. In the permit example, the agreed supply condition has not been established just because the buyer believes approval is likely. The actual approval contemplated by the agreement matters. This protects the conditional nature of the commitment. It does not mean that the parties never made any agreement or that all connected duties disappear before approval. For instance, a separately agreed obligation to cooperate in the application may need independent consideration. The condition controls the obligation to which it is attached, not necessarily every duty arising from the transaction.

    Section 513(2) provides a further rule where the event concerns a person's future act and that person acts so as to make the stipulated act impossible. The inquiry is about the specified act in its setting, rather than every abstract possibility that might be imagined. This helps distinguish waiting for an uncertain choice from a situation where the required event can no longer occur. A factual answer must identify the person's relevant conduct and explain its effect. Without those facts, announcing impossibility would be an unsupported conclusion about the very event on which the obligation depends.

    **Negative contingency**

    Under section 513(3), an obligation dependent on an uncertain event not happening arises when that event becomes impossible. Absence alone is not always impossibility. A delayed return can still occur unless something establishes that the stipulated event can no longer happen. Suppose the condition concerns return of a unique identified item. Its confirmed destruction may establish non-return in a way that mere lateness does not. The example explains the need for a legal and factual threshold. Otherwise a claimant could decide for themselves that waiting has lasted long enough and demand performance before the condition agreed by the parties was actually satisfied.

    The rule also shows why certainty and evidence are important. The agreement should identify the event sufficiently clearly to permit a conclusion about its occurrence or impossibility. A broad claim that circumstances have changed does not identify whether the negative contingency has matured. The parties may dispute what return, approval or completion means in context. Section 515's approach to interpretation assists in resolving the meaning of the term, while the evidence establishes what actually happened. Interpretation and proof perform different functions. Neither a clear condition without proven facts nor strong facts unrelated to the chosen condition supplies a complete claim.

    **Fixed-time conditions**

    Section 513(4) deals with an event required within fixed time and its impossibility in that setting. A permit required by a specified deadline is different from a permit that may be obtained at any future time. Approval outside the agreed window does not automatically satisfy a requirement tied to that window. The precise condition must be read. If a party wants late approval to count, that may require an agreement or construction supporting that result. Courts should not be assumed to rewrite the deadline simply because later performance would still be commercially convenient to the person seeking it.

    Section 513(5) addresses an event not happening within fixed time. It recognises non-occurrence within that time and certainty that it will not occur within the window. A consignment absent on Monday may still arrive before Friday. A demonstrated impossibility of Friday arrival is a different fact. The timeline therefore helps identify whether the condition remains uncertain, is satisfied or cannot be satisfied. These rules make fixed-time conditions practically useful: the parties can connect their obligations with an identifiable period instead of leaving a claim dependent on indefinite speculation about everything that might happen later.

    **Later impossibility of performance**

    Section 531 concerns an existing contractual performance made impossible by a fundamental change in circumstances. That is conceptually different from an obligation awaiting its agreed contingency. If A unconditionally promised delivery and later an essential subject is destroyed, section 531 may require examination. If A promised delivery only on approval and approval never occurs, section 513 may be the immediate issue. The event's label does not settle which section applies. The original allocation of obligations and risks matters. A student should therefore avoid treating every permit problem, delay or destroyed item as the same kind of conditional contract.

    Section 531 also has exclusions and accounting consequences which should not be transferred mechanically into section 513. Mere difficulty or reduced profit is not the same as the statutory fundamental change. Conversely, a failed contingency should not automatically be described as the other party's wrongful breach. An independent promise to secure or pursue the event may raise a separate issue, but that promise must be established. In conclusion, positive events, negative events, deadlines and later impossibility answer different questions. Accurate interpretation and a supported timeline allow Nepal's provisions to operate distinctly, producing a more reliable conclusion than a general statement that uncertainty always suspends or destroys every contractual duty.

## Test yourself

??? question "Does waiting for a permit always mean no agreement exists?"
    No. An agreement may exist while its relevant performance obligation is conditional.

??? question "Is absence on Monday enough to prove non-arrival by Friday?"
    Not by itself. Apply the fixed-time rule and the evidence.

??? question "Which provision governs an event not happening without fixed time?"
    Section 513(3), focusing on impossibility of the event happening.

??? question "Is every conditional payment a wager?"
    No. Examine the purpose and legality separately.

??? question "Does a condition automatically erase a separate promise to apply for approval?"
    No. Identify and interpret that independent promise.

??? question "How many subsections does the checked 513 have?"
    Five. Do not invent an extra subsection for initial impossibility.

## One-minute revision

- Identify agreement, event, duty and time.
- Happening and non-happening require different analysis.
- Not yet happened is not always impossible.
- Fixed-time conditions need the actual deadline.
- Contingency differs from reward acceptance and later impossibility.
- All ordinary validity requirements still apply.
