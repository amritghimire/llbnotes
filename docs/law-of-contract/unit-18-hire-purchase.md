# Unit 18: Hire-purchase

!!! abstract "In one line"
    Hire-purchase gives possession and use now, while ownership passes after the last instalment under the Code; return, default and repossession have separate safeguards.

## 18.1 Meaning, parties and essentials

Section 624 concerns movable goods let for possession and use with periodic instalments, a purchase right under the terms, ownership after the last instalment and the hirer's pre-transfer termination right. The contract must be **written**, and the owner must give a copy free of charge promptly. The hirer's definition includes the statutory successor/recipient. A credit sale does not become hire-purchase simply because its price is payable in instalments.

## 18.2 Required contents and connected agreements

Section 625 requires description, hire-purchase price, cash price, start date, instalment amounts/number, due dates, payment method and recipient/place. Missing content does not mean every contract is automatically void: the hirer may sue, and the court can rescind or make an appropriate order where the omission is detrimental. Under s 626 the specified connected agreements, including financier/surety arrangements, are treated as one for the chapter; the last date governs where they were made on different dates.

## 18.3 Implied safeguards and transfer

Section 627 supplies uninterrupted possession/use, freedom from the stated charges, comparison opportunity, right to sell, satisfactory quality and purpose-related fitness in its stated terms. Its quality exceptions include the owner's lack of knowledge of the defect, stipulated defects, relevant sample correspondence and identified second-hand goods. Do not suppress those qualifications or read them as cancelling every separate description undertaking.

Section 628 transfers ownership on payment of the **last instalment**; possession/use begins on receipt. Section 629 treats the goods as bailed until ownership passes, requires reasonable care unless otherwise arranged and makes the hirer responsible for loss from failure of care. See [bailment](unit-13-bailment-pledge.md).

## 18.4 Hirer's duties and rights

Pay instalments as agreed (s 630), use the goods consistently with the contract (s 634) and supply the requested location/condition information within 15 days under s 635. Section 633 permits transfer of the hirer's rights/interests/liabilities with owner consent; the owner has 15 days to respond, silence counts as refusal, and the hirer has the stated 35-day court route against unreasonable refusal. Do not treat this as a power to sell the owner's complete title.

Subject to contrary terms, s 631 allows early completion by payment with at least 15 days' prior written notice, including the applicable discount. Section 632 allows return-based termination before the last instalment with at least 15 days' notice in its stated setting. Return the goods and settle the applicable amount. The half-price provision limits the instalment-price balance as stated, but **s 632(4) preserves other contractual amounts, charges or dues**. It is not a universal release from all loss or charges.

Example: with a Rs 120,000 hire-purchase price, Rs 45,000 paid would leave Rs 15,000 to reach half under the stated s 632(3) rule, before considering s 632(4) and the applicable terms. Do not present that example as the only sum possible in every early termination.

## 18.5 Owner's termination and protection of the hirer

Under s 636(1), **more than one** unpaid instalment triggers the stated termination power, with one week's advance notice for weekly-or-shorter intervals and 15 days otherwise. Cure by paying instalments and contractual interest before notice expires prevents that termination under subsection (2). Other stated breaches require the relevant 15-day notice under subsection (3).

Subsection (4) concerns recovery/forfeiture, accounting and the stated police-assisted entry; it is not permission for private violence. Subsection (5) prevents termination under the section after at least the basic price has been paid by the hirer, surety or investor: **three-fourths where the total price is up to Rs 50,000; at least half where it exceeds Rs 50,000**. Contravening that protection has the release/refund consequence in subsection (6), with goods returned. Subsection (7) preserves a price claim. **Subsection (8) separately preserves termination for failure to provide s 635 information**; do not call the threshold absolute immunity.

## 18.6 Accounting, insolvency and limitation

Section 637 refers to the amount already paid toward the hire-purchase price and the value of the goods on the forfeiture day. Its explanation defines that value through sale proceeds less the stated deductions. It prescribes a net-value calculation with reasonable seizure, storage/repair, sale and statutory-dues deductions, and payment within 30 days; delayed payment attracts the checked 10% annual interest rule. Its precise language is unusual; do not substitute a familiar foreign formula or an invented depreciation percentage. Section 638 deals with the liquidator's role and court-approved transfer in the hirer's insolvency. Section 639 gives two years from accrual for chapter claims.

## Full 20-mark answers

These are full practice answers. Adapt the emphasis to the question; the paragraph headings help you organise a 35-minute answer.

??? example "Q1. Explain hire-purchase, its essentials and the hirer’s rights and duties. (20 marks)"
    !!! warning "Unverified"

    **Introduction**

    Hire-purchase allows a person to use movable goods while paying periodically under a purchase arrangement. Its practical attraction is immediate use without paying the whole purchase amount at once. Its legal structure is important: possession and use can begin before ownership passes. Nepal's Civil Code regulates the arrangement in sections 624 to 639. The chapter should not be replaced with a generic description of buying on credit. A credit sale and hire-purchase may both involve instalments, but ownership, early return and default are governed by different rules. Those differences determine the parties' rights.

    **Meaning and parties**

    Section 624 identifies the arrangement through periodic payments, the hirer's right to possess and use, the contractual purchase right, ownership on the last instalment and the ability to terminate before ownership transfers. The owner provides the goods; the hirer receives them under those conditions. The chapter's goods are movable property capable of being hired for possession and use. The statutory definition of hirer also includes the relevant successor or recipient. These details make it possible to distinguish the chapter from an ordinary lease, where payment for use does not itself lead to ownership.

    The contract must be in writing, and the owner must supply a copy free of charge promptly. This is a legal safeguard rather than an optional convenience. The document allows the hirer to know the total cost, due dates and termination terms and helps establish the actual arrangement in a dispute. A seller's informal description of monthly payment does not resolve whether section 624 applies. The substance must meet the statutory structure. A student should identify the goods, parties, ownership condition and ending rights before discussing default or recovery.

    **Required information and connected transactions**

    Section 625 requires the description, hire-purchase price, cash price, starting date, instalment amounts and number, payment dates, mode and recipient or place. The difference between cash price and hire-purchase price is particularly useful because the hirer should understand the total amount needed to acquire ownership. An initial deposit forms part of the defined hire-purchase price in the stated setting. Omission of required information does not automatically make every arrangement void. The hirer can seek relief, and the court may rescind or make an appropriate order where the omission is detrimental.

    Section 626 deals with the specified connected agreements, including arrangements involving a surety or financier, as a single contract for the chapter. Where the connected instruments have different dates, the latest date is treated as the formation date under the provision. This prevents the analysis from focusing only on the hire document while ignoring the linked structure covered by the statute. However, a student should establish that the agreement falls within the section rather than treat every unrelated financial dealing between the parties as part of the same transaction.

    **Quality and possession safeguards**

    Section 627 supplies important terms concerning uninterrupted possession and use, freedom from the stated charges, opportunity to compare with a sample, the owner's right to sell, satisfactory quality and fitness for the communicated purpose in the statutory setting. These safeguards protect the benefit the hirer is paying to receive. The section also contains specific quality qualifications, including the owner's lack of knowledge of the defect, stipulated defects, relevant sample correspondence and identified second-hand goods. The answer must preserve those qualifications instead of presenting every assurance as unconditional.

    Description and sample must be considered according to their own rules. A qualification concerning quality does not necessarily erase a separately promised description. If the agreement concerns a particular machine, the owner cannot assume that delivering a different machine settles the obligation merely because it operates. The legal question is what the statute and terms require. A clear answer distinguishes entitlement to use, quality, fitness and the right to acquire ownership. They are connected aspects of performance but do not always have the same factual trigger or qualification.

    **Ownership, care and payment**

    Section 628 passes ownership on completion through payment of the last instalment. Possession and use arise on receipt. Section 629 treats the goods as bailed before ownership passes and requires reasonable care and maintenance unless the contract provides otherwise. The hirer is responsible for loss caused by failure of the required care. Thus a person using a hired machine cannot assume that the owner's retained title excuses reckless treatment. Equally, the owner cannot describe every ordinary deterioration as negligent damage without considering the actual duty and facts.

    Section 630 requires payment by the agreed period and method. Section 634 concerns use contrary to the contract, while section 635 requires the requested location and condition information within fifteen days. Information protects the owner's continuing interest while the goods are outside the owner's possession. The hirer's right to transfer rights, interests or liabilities under section 633 requires consent and includes the stated response and court-review routes. It is not equivalent to unrestricted power to sell the owner's complete title. The transferred interest must remain within what the hirer lawfully holds.

    **Early completion, return and conclusion**

    Subject to contrary terms, section 631 allows early completion by paying the relevant price or outstanding instalments with at least fifteen days' written notice and the applicable discount. Section 632 provides return-based termination before the last instalment with the required notice, return and payment. Its half-price provision concerns the stated price balance, while subsection (4) preserves other contractual amounts, charges or dues. The hirer should not be told that returning the goods necessarily erases every liability. The contractual terms and statutory qualifications remain part of the calculation.

    Sections 636 to 639 then address owner termination, accounting, insolvency and the chapter's two-year cause-of-action period. The rules show that retained ownership and immediate use must be balanced. In conclusion, hire-purchase is a structured relationship involving written information, delayed ownership, care and payment obligations, and defined routes for ending early. A strong answer starts with the separation of possession and ownership, explains each statutory safeguard and distinguishes a genuine hire-purchase from credit sale or ordinary lease. Payment frequency alone is never a sufficient legal classification.

??? example "Q2. Discuss default, termination and protection against wrongful repossession. (20 marks)"

    **Introduction**

    Default in hire-purchase creates competing interests. The owner has not yet transferred ownership and expects the agreed payments. The hirer may already have paid a substantial amount and depend on continued use of the goods. Nepal's Civil Code does not resolve the problem simply by allowing the owner to take everything after any missed payment. Sections 631 and 632 regulate hirer-initiated ending, while section 636 governs the owner's termination and contains important safeguards. Section 637 addresses accounting after the stated forfeiture. These provisions must be read together rather than reduced to an unrestricted ownership claim.

    **Start with the contract and ownership position**

    Section 624 defines the hire-purchase arrangement and requires writing. Section 625 specifies required contents, including instalments and payment dates. Section 628 postpones ownership until payment of the last instalment, while possession and use begin on receipt. The legal relationship is therefore different from an ordinary sale where ownership may already have passed despite an unpaid credit price. Before discussing repossession, the student should establish that the transaction really is hire-purchase, what instalments are due, what was paid and which breach the owner relies on. The remedy depends on those facts.

    **Non-payment and notice**

    Section 636(1) addresses default in more than one instalment. That threshold should not be replaced with a statement that one missed payment always permits termination. The notice period depends on the payment interval: one week for weekly or shorter intervals and fifteen days in the other cases stated. Notice gives the hirer a chance to respond before the relationship ends. The answer should identify the actual payment schedule and notice date. An owner cannot establish compliance merely by saying that a warning was issued at some unspecified time.

    Subsection (2) protects cure before the notice period ends. Payment of the due instalment and the contractual interest, if applicable, prevents the termination based on that notice as provided. This shows why timing matters. A hirer who pays within the allowed period is in a different position from one who ignores the notice until after a lawful termination. The provision should not be confused with a universal power to revive any ended arrangement on any later date. State the particular notice, cure and statutory consequence rather than make an unlimited claim.

    **Other breaches and the recovery route**

    Section 636(3) addresses the relevant failure to comply with the contract or the care and information obligations in sections 629 and 635, with at least fifteen days' notice. Not every default is therefore a missed instalment. Misuse or failure to provide required information may raise a separate ground. The student should distinguish the applicable subsection and preserve its notice requirement. The fact that ownership remains with the owner does not make every contractual safeguard irrelevant. Retained title explains the continuing interest, but the Code regulates how that interest may be exercised.

    Subsection (4) concerns taking back or forfeiting the goods, accounting for sums and the stated police-assisted entry. It should not be described as authorising private violence or arbitrary seizure. A legal recovery route and a creditor's personal decision are different things. The owner must also identify any lawful deductions or compensation claim rather than treat every amount already paid as automatically forfeited. The particular statutory provisions governing retained sums, deposit and loss must be applied. Accounting is central because repossession affects both the goods and the value already contributed by the hirer.

    **Basic price protection**

    Section 636(5) limits termination after at least the basic price has been paid by the hirer, surety or investor. In the checked text, the threshold is three-fourths where the total hire-purchase price is up to fifty thousand rupees, and at least half where it is more than fifty thousand. These are statutory thresholds, not estimates of how much use the hirer has enjoyed. A problem answer should state the total price and amount paid, perform the calculation and then examine the relevant statutory qualifications. A vague claim that substantial payment is enough is less useful.

    Subsection (6) addresses termination contrary to that protection, releasing the stated liabilities and requiring refund of the entire amount paid, while the goods must be returned. Subsection (7) preserves the owner's price claim despite the restriction on termination. This distinction is important: limiting repossession does not necessarily erase the remaining price obligation. Subsection (8) also preserves termination in the stated failure-to-provide-information situation under section 635. Consequently, the threshold should not be described as absolute immunity from every termination ground. The subsections must be read together and the particular breach identified.

    **The hirer's own ending rights**

    Sections 631 and 632 provide different routes initiated by the hirer. Early completion involves payment of the price or outstanding instalments with the required advance written notice, subject to the terms and applicable discount. Return-based termination involves giving the statutory notice before the last instalment, returning the goods and meeting the stated payment obligations. The half-price rule in section 632(3) must be read with subsection (4), which preserves other contractual amounts, charges or dues. A hirer should not assume that leaving the goods outside the owner's premises automatically ends all responsibility.

    **Post-forfeiture accounting and conclusion**

    Section 637 prescribes the hirer's entitlement following the specified forfeiture and defines the relevant net value by reference to proceeds less reasonable seizure, storage or repair, sale and statutory-dues expenses. The text's wording should not be replaced with an invented depreciation formula. It also sets a thirty-day payment period and the checked ten-percent annual interest rule for delay. Section 638 addresses the liquidator's role on insolvency, while section 639 gives two years from accrual for chapter claims. These provisions show that recovery is not the end of the legal analysis.

    In conclusion, hire-purchase default requires careful attention to instalment count, notice, cure, care, information and the amount already paid. The owner has a protected interest but must use the statutory route and account properly. The hirer has significant safeguards but remains subject to the contract and the Code's express exceptions. A complete examination answer explains both sides, distinguishes ending authority from continuing price claims and avoids suggesting either automatic confiscation after one missed payment or complete immunity after crossing a payment threshold.

## Test yourself

??? question "Does monthly payment alone prove hire-purchase?"
    No. Check s 624's structure, including delayed ownership and pre-transfer termination.

??? question "When does ownership pass under s 628?"
    On payment of the last instalment; possession and use begin earlier on receipt.

??? question "Does one missed instalment trigger s 636(1)?"
    The checked provision requires more than one unpaid instalment and the applicable notice.

??? question "Does the basic-price threshold erase the price claim?"
    No. Section 636(7) preserves that claim, and subsection (8) has an information exception.

??? question "Does return erase every charge?"
    No. Read s 632(4) alongside the half-price provision.

??? question "Can the owner withhold the contract copy unless paid for it?"
    Section 624 requires a free copy promptly.

## One-minute revision

- Written agreement and free copy are mandatory.
- Cash price and total hire-purchase price differ.
- Possession begins on receipt; ownership follows the last instalment.
- Care, payment, authorised use and information remain duties.
- Default requires the proper subsection, notice and cure analysis.
- Payment thresholds restrict termination but have express qualifications.
