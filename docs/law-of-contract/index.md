# Law of Contract (Law 305)

**100 marks, pass 35. Compulsory. TU LL.B. first year, new course 2076/077 B.S.**

Contract law asks when an agreement creates a legal duty, what the parties must do and what happens if one side fails. A shop's order for desks, a vehicle left for repair and a person authorised to buy stock raise different questions. Start by identifying the people, their promise, the applicable law and the remedy sought.

The paper has **19 teaching units and 10 prescribed cases**. Units 1–11 explain general principles; Units 12–19 cover special transactions and the Civil Code structure. The cases form syllabus item 20. Their numbers below preserve the syllabus order.

Nepal's main source is **Muluki Civil Code 2074, Part 5**. Indian and English textbook rules help explain concepts, but they do not automatically govern Nepal. For example, Code s 505 lists consent, capacity, a sufficiently certain obligation and a lawful obligation; it does not separately list consideration in the same way as the Indian textbook framework. Read [Unit 1](unit-01-introduction.md) and [Unit 3](unit-03-consideration-privity.md) together before memorising a foreign essentials list.

## How to study this paper

1. Start with [what a contract is](unit-01-introduction.md), then [offer and acceptance](unit-02-offer-acceptance.md), [capacity](unit-04-capacity.md), [free consent](unit-05-free-consent.md) and [legality](unit-06-legality.md). These answer whether an enforceable obligation exists.
2. Read [consideration and privity](unit-03-consideration-privity.md) with the Nepal/India/England distinction in view. Privity asks who can enforce the promise, not merely who benefits from it.
3. Move to [performance](unit-09-performance.md), [termination](unit-10-termination.md) and [remedies](unit-11-breach-remedies.md). Keep ending future performance separate from compensation or return of benefits already received.
4. Prioritise [agency](unit-14-agency.md) and [bailment/pledge](unit-13-bailment-pledge.md), then complete the remaining special-contract units. A topic not present in the two available papers is still in the syllabus.
5. Read a case after its linked unit. Close the page and explain its facts, issue, actual order and reason for the order. Then check the distinction between the historical rule and today's separate comparison.
6. Use the unit's full answers for timed writing, the self-tests for recall and the one-minute revision bullets before the exam. See [How to write answers](../how-to-answer.md).

## Exam guide

For a long doctrinal answer, define the topic in ordinary words, identify the Nepal provision and explain its conditions. Apply a short example, distinguish the relevant foreign rule and conclude on the actual question. An answer about bailment should explain custody and purpose before listing duties; an answer about an unpaid seller must distinguish Indian statutory rights from Nepal's verified rules.

For a case answer, use **citation → material facts → issues → arguments → decision → ratio → comment**. The decision is what the court ordered; the ratio decidendi is the legal reason supporting that result. Do not treat a party's allegation, a lower court's reasoning or permission to appeal as the Supreme Court's final finding. In 20.10, for example, the game could meet the historical gambling definition, yet the defendants remained acquitted because the charged stakes were not proved.

In a three-hour paper requiring five answers, allow roughly 35 minutes per long answer and reserve time to choose questions and check omissions. Adapt to the instructions printed on your paper. A short note needs a definition, core conditions, Nepal's position and an example or distinction; it should not consume the time for a whole long answer. The [unit answers](unit-01-introduction.md#full-20-mark-answers) show connected exam prose rather than a list of headings.
