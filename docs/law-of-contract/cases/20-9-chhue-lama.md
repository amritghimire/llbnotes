## Facts

After Taranath's death, his widow Mithu Devi and children were living together. Mithu Devi sold the house/property. The minor son Rajendra's adult sister Ishwari Devi acted to protect his interest. The court examined her standing (right to bring the case) where the ordinary guardian's own act was challenged and whether the sale was justified as household management.

The Full Bench considered the effect of the historical property shares and the sale. It found the sale could not be treated as the asserted household-management transaction on the facts described. It upheld invalidation of **half** the transfer, preserving the mother's then-recognised share (paras 14–15).

## Issues

1. Could the co-resident adult sister protect the minor brother where their mother's act conflicted with his interests?
2. Was the sale justified as household management?
3. What part of the transfer could stand under the historical shares applied?

## Arguments

**Chhechyu/defence position:** the mother as household head could make the transaction, and objections were raised to representation and cancellation.

**Minor's side:** the sale harmed the children and was not the asserted household-management necessity; an adult sister could seek effective protection where the guardian herself caused the conflict. The claim sought a greater cancelled portion than the half ultimately upheld.

## Decision

The Full Bench upheld the regional court's cancellation of **one-half** of the transfer, leaving the other half effective under the historical share analysis (para 15). It also explained the protective standing principle and noted the earlier final ruling allowing the sister to represent her brother (para 14).

## Ratio decidendi

Our English paraphrase of the official headnote and reasons, not an official translation:

1. A mother would ordinarily be the natural guardian in the stated family setting, but that does not block effective relief where her own dealings harm the minor's interest. An adult co-resident sibling may protect that interest (para 14).
2. On the facts, selling the residence and leaving the children in the described position was not justified as the asserted household-management transaction (para 15).
3. The mother's own then-recognised share could stand; the minor's half was protected. The historic share calculation is not a statement of today's rights of sons, daughters or spouses (para 15).

## Why it matters

This case is about **protection and conflict of interest**, not merely a minor's signature. The person who normally represents a child may be the person whose transaction must be challenged. The decision explains why representation cannot be used to make effective protection impossible.
