## Facts

Birat Bahadur Khadka received Rs 750,000 from Prithvi Bahadur Maharjan and gave an advance writing on 2052/11/23. It contemplated transfer within nine months after the specified land was registered in Birat's elder brother Shambhu's name. The registration occurred on 2057/1/30, but the promised transfer did not follow. Prithvi sued on 2057/11/9 for return of the advance.

The District Court allowed recovery. The Appellate Court dismissed the suit on limitation. The Supreme Court examined both the legal classification of the advance receipt and when a claim arose under its terms (paras 14–17).

## Issues

1. Did this advance receipt attract Contract Act 2023 limitation or the historical Len Den Byabahar framework?
2. Did time run from the old receipt alone, or after the promised later transfer became due and was not performed?

## Arguments

**Prithvi:** the claim was timely under Len Den Byabahar No. 40 and the money should be returned.

**Birat:** the writing should be treated under Contract Act 2023 and the claim rejected on the corresponding limitation argument. The judgment also records Shambhu's denial that another person's writing bound him; that was not a finding authorising transfer of his title.

## Decision

The Supreme Court reversed the Appellate Court and restored the District Court's recovery of **Rs 750,000** from Birat. The suit was within the applicable historical limitation period calculated in relation to the transfer becoming due (paras 15–17).

## Ratio decidendi

Our English paraphrase of the official headnote and reasons, not an official translation:

1. The court characterised the particular one-sided advance receipt as a transaction document governed by Len Den Byabahar, not a Contract Act 2023 contract for the limitation question (para 15).
2. Its promised transfer depended on later registration followed by nine months. The cause for suit arose when performance due under that arrangement did not occur, rather than automatically on the receipt date (para 16).
3. The historical two-year No. 40 period therefore did not bar the suit (para 17). This does not establish that every unsigned or one-signature modern agreement is invalid.

## Why it matters

Limitation requires two questions: **which provision applies**, and **what event starts time**? This case is useful because it answers both from the transaction's substance and the specific promised sequence, rather than merely counting years from the oldest paper.

### Then and now

| Then, as discussed in the judgment | Present comparison and limit |
|---|---|
| Historical classification of this receipt and No. 40 limitation | Current classification and the applicable limitation provision must be identified afresh. There is no universal current “all advances: two years” rule established here. |
| Discussion of signatures in the historical reasoning | Code s 505 allows written, oral or conduct-based formation subject to mandatory form. Do not turn the old receipt analysis into a two-signature rule for all current contracts. |
