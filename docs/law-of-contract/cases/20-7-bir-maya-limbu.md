## Facts

Ran Maya claimed recovery on a writing dated 2052/6/12 concerning Rs 680,000. The instrument included repayment of principal and interest and wording concerning a possible land transfer. Bir Maya denied that the signature/thumb impression was hers and argued that the claimant had chosen the wrong legal route.

A handwriting/fingerprint expert examined the disputed writing and testified in support of the opinion. The Supreme Court considered both the instrument's actual repayment terms and that evidence (paras 13–16).

## Issues

1. Did the alternative land wording force the claimant to sue only under contract law rather than claim the money?
2. Was execution of the writing proved despite the denial?

## Arguments

**Bir Maya:** the document was not made as the asserted transaction-law instrument, the claim should have been under the Contract Act, and her denial required proper proof.

**Ran Maya:** the money terms supported principal/interest recovery, and expert examination supported by testimony established the disputed execution.

## Decision

Recovery of principal and interest was upheld; Bir Maya's appeal failed (para 16). The court did not need to order the alternative land transfer to uphold the money claim.

## Ratio decidendi

Our English paraphrase of the official headnote and reasons, not an official translation:

1. The instrument itself permitted the money claim and contained the relevant repayment and interest terms. Alternative land wording did not make the money remedy unavailable (para 14).
2. The expert's opinion, supported by testimony, was admissible under the historical Evidence Act provisions discussed and supported the finding of execution (para 15).
3. The decision rested on the writing and supporting evidence; expert opinion was not described as automatically infallible in every case (para 16).

## Why it matters

Read the whole document before choosing a legal category or remedy. A single land-related clause did not erase express money terms. Proof of the disputed signature was a separate issue from classification, and the judgment addressed both.
