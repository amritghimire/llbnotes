## Facts

Advocate Achyut Prasad Kharel challenged provisions of the Muluki Ain allowing detention in the debt/enforcement settings identified in his petition. This was a public-law challenge, not his personal suit to recover a purchase price. He relied on the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights (ICCPR), Article 11, which protects against imprisonment merely for inability to fulfil a contractual obligation, and Nepal Treaty Act 2047 s 9(1).

The challenged provisions included Danda Sajaya No. 42(2), Len Den Byabahar No. 19, Damasahi No. 10 and Baki Natirne No. 10. The court first asked whether the obligations covered by those provisions could all be classified as contractual (paras 6–8).

## Issues

1. Were all the challenged civil liabilities contractual obligations for the petition's ICCPR argument?
2. Should the court invalidate the provisions as requested?

## Arguments

**Petitioner:** the detention provisions conflicted with ICCPR Article 11 and should be declared invalid through Treaty Act s 9(1) and the then Constitution's Article 88(1).

**Respondents:** civil obligations arise from sources other than contracts. The petition wrongly grouped different statutory liabilities together as contractual. The judgment records additional treaty arguments in the replies; do not assume the court adopted every respondent argument.

## Decision

The petition was dismissed (para 9). The court rejected the premise that every civil liability in the challenged provisions was a contractual liability. It did not grant the requested declaration invalidating the provisions.

## Ratio decidendi

Our English paraphrase of the official headnote and reasons, not an official translation:

1. Contract law is a particular branch within civil law. The judgment described the elements needed for a lawful contract under the historical framework (para 7).
2. Contractual liability arises from a contract satisfying the applicable contract law. A duty arising under another law is not automatically contractual because it is civil or involves money (para 8).
3. The petition's wholesale classification failed, so the requested declaration did not follow (para 9). This is not a holding that every form of debt imprisonment is lawful in every circumstance.

## Why it matters

Use this case to explain why an agreement, a contract and a civil obligation are different ideas. Its useful method is to identify the **source** of the duty before choosing the remedy. The historical classification should be reported accurately even if a modern statute organises obligations differently.
