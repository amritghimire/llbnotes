## Facts

An arbitral tribunal made an award in a dispute involving the Sunsari Morang Irrigation Project and Larsen & Toubro Ltd. The Appellate Court Patan upheld the award on 2059/8/5. Larsen & Toubro applied to Kathmandu District Court for enforcement on 2059/9/29.

The project attacked enforcement as late, arguing that the Act's 45-day compliance and following 30-day application periods should be counted from the tribunal award even while the statutory challenge was pending. It also relied on an asserted further writ proceeding. The Supreme Court examined the relationship between the award-challenge provision and the enforcement provisions, and the actual enforcement application (paras 8–19).

## Issues

1. How should historical ss 30, 31 and 32 operate together when an award is challenged before the Appellate Court?
2. Was this enforcement application late on the court's construction?
3. Should the challenged enforcement steps be quashed?

## Arguments

**Project:** the application was beyond the periods calculated from the original award; enforcement was improper and should be stopped.

**Larsen & Toubro:** the statutory challenge meant the award was not yet final in the relevant sense; the Act should be read coherently, with enforcement time following finality rather than expiring during that review. The company opposed the writ.

## Decision

The writ was **dismissed**. On its historical construction, the Supreme Court treated the enforcement application after the Appellate Court's decision as timely and found no defect justifying the requested interference (paras 18–19). Do not reverse the result merely because the reasoning discusses waiting for finality.

## Ratio decidendi

Our English paraphrase of the official headnote and reasons, not an official translation:

1. The statutory challenge and enforcement provisions were interconnected and should be read harmoniously, not so that one made the other ineffective (para 16).
2. In the statutory setting then considered, enforcement responsibility/time followed the award becoming final after the relevant Appellate Court review (paras 16–17).
3. The actual enforcement application fell within the resulting 45-plus-30-day framework; the court therefore refused the project relief (paras 18–19).
4. This historical finality reasoning is not a verified current rule that every challenge or writ automatically stays enforcement.

    **Introduction and citation**

    The prescribed arbitration case is identified reliably by D.N. 7836. The syllabus calls it Anil Krishna Pokhrel v Kathmandu District Court and gives NKP 2064 issue 5. The public judgment names authorised project chief Anil Kumar Pokharel and reports issue 4. The Supreme Court decided it on 2063/9/25 through Justices Anup Raj Sharma and Sharada Prasad Pandit, with Justice Sharma writing. An answer should preserve that difference rather than invent a second judgment or silently correct the source. The case concerns the historical relationship between challenging an award and enforcing it.

    **Material facts**

    The dispute involved an award against the Sunsari Morang Irrigation Project in favour of Larsen & Toubro Ltd. The award was examined under the Arbitration Act 2055, and the Appellate Court Patan upheld it on 2059/8/5. The company then applied to Kathmandu District Court for enforcement on 2059/9/29. The project challenged the enforcement proceedings. Its central argument was that the statutory time had already expired because the compliance and enforcement periods should have been calculated from the tribunal's award, without waiting for the statutory award challenge to be decided.

    The project also referred to a further writ proceeding and asked for interference with the enforcement orders. The Supreme Court therefore had to consider both the Act's structure and the actual application before the District Court. An examination account should not state merely that an award existed or that proceedings were pending. It should identify which challenge occurred, when the Appellate Court dealt with it and when enforcement was requested. Those events explain the limitation argument and the court's response. The exact award amount is unnecessary to understand this ratio and need not be guessed from imperfect transcription.

    **Issues and arguments**

    The first question was whether sections 30, 31 and 32 should be read separately or as connected parts of the same statutory process. The second was whether the company's application was timely under the resulting interpretation. The project emphasised section 31's forty-five-day compliance period and section 32's following thirty-day application period. On its account, counting from the original award made the later application too late. It argued that the statutory review did not justify waiting. The company responded that a challenge capable of setting aside or returning the award for decision affected its finality.

    Larsen & Toubro's position was that the review and enforcement provisions should not be interpreted to defeat each other. Requiring enforcement steps while the same award remained under the statutory challenge could create an impractical and inconsistent scheme. The question was therefore not simply whether a party preferred speed or delay. It was how the legislature's connected provisions could operate coherently. A useful answer explains this conflict before giving the interpretive principle, because the principle becomes clearer when tied to the procedural problem the court actually had to resolve.

    **Harmonious interpretation**

    Paragraph 16 treats the award-challenge and enforcement provisions as interconnected. Harmonious interpretation means reading related provisions so that each retains a workable role. A court should not isolate one deadline in a manner that makes another statutory right ineffective. The judgment reasoned that section 30 gave the Appellate Court power to examine the award and, in the stated circumstances, set it aside or require a further decision. That possibility mattered to the historical finality analysis. It was not enough to say that an arbitral document had been issued and therefore every later procedural question was settled.

    Paragraph 17 develops the practical consequence. In the statutory setting then considered, enforcement responsibility and the related time framework followed the award becoming final after the relevant review. The court rejected an interpretation requiring the party to pursue execution within the original calculation while the award remained under that challenge. This was its construction of the provisions as they stood and were argued in the case. It should be reported as a historical legal conclusion, not converted into an unlimited rule that any application bearing the word challenge defeats every form of enforcement.

    **Application and actual result**

    Paragraph 18 applies the reasoning to the dates. The Appellate Court upheld the award on 2059/8/5, and the enforcement request was filed on 2059/9/29. The Supreme Court treated the request as within the resulting forty-five-plus-thirty-day framework. Paragraph 19 consequently found no defect justifying the requested writ and dismissed it. This result is important. The court discussed finality, but it did not grant the project an order permanently preventing enforcement. A student who repeats only the headnote's statement about waiting could accidentally reverse the outcome of the case.

    The case also does not establish a verified general rule that every pending Supreme Court writ automatically stays execution. The project's assertions must be distinguished from the court's actual construction and dismissal. The relevant historical holding concerns the statutory award-review process and the enforcement application before it. This distinction protects accurate use of precedent: a ratio must connect the material facts, the legal rule and the order made. It cannot be enlarged simply because one party raised a broader argument in the petition or because a phrase can be read in isolation.

    **Then and now**

    Later amendments create a separate study issue. The finished Procedural Law ADR notes record amended section 32(2)'s no-automatic-stay rule and section 32(3)'s separate High Court stay route. Unit 17 cross-checks that recorded position but warns that the fresh consolidated PDF could not be retrieved. Current applicability and later changes remain unverified here. Historical explanation and present comparison require separate statements and separate sources.

    The case's continuing teaching value is its method: identify connected provisions, preserve their operation and apply the chosen construction to the actual procedural dates. An examination answer should give the citation discrepancy, facts, competing timing arguments, harmonious interpretation, timely application and dismissal, then add the amendment caution. That sequence explains both the principle and its limits.
