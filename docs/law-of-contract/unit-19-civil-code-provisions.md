## 19.2 Lease: meaning and formation

Section 610 concerns possession, use and benefits from the lessor's property for periodic rent during a term. Consumable goods that disappear through use cannot be leased under the stated rule. The lessor ordinarily must provide the goods in usable condition and secure peaceful use unless otherwise arranged. Lease gives use, not an automatic purchase right.

### Use, repair and responsibility

Section 611 restricts changing the original form without consent, while preserving the stated repair/improvement allowance. Section 612 requires good-faith, prudent, purpose-consistent use. Under s 613 the lessor ordinarily repairs; an immediately necessary repair may be undertaken by the lessee after informing the lessor, with reimbursement or rent adjustment as stated.

Section 614 requires prompt information about damage, unfitness or interference; the lessor has the stated 15-day restoration obligation, with proportional rent reduction where restoration cannot be completed. Section 615 requires protection, restoration/compensation for the stated damage, but excludes the defined force-majeure situation and addresses reduced usability/rent. It does not justify the claim that any event beyond convenience excuses every duty.

### Rent, term and sublease

Section 616 applies agreed rent terms; its default dates differ for movable property, specified land and other property. It also permits the stated proportional reduction for interference with full use. Section 617 sets category-based maximum terms: housing/construction and servitude land 35 years; industrial/infrastructure land 40; farming land 20; other house/land 19; the listed vehicle/machinery categories 15; domestic animals and remaining goods 10, subject to the exact category and renewal provisions. These are checked historical figures, not a universal lease maximum.

Section 618 requires prior consent for sublease, keeps the original lessee's obligations and prevents a sublease exceeding the main term. Direct sublessee liability requires the relevant arrangement; it should not be assumed for every claim.

### Return, immovable property and termination

Section 619 ordinarily requires return within 35 days of ending, with the stated immovable-property consequence and ordinary deterioration exceptions. Return the recorded accessories subject to the statutory qualifications. Section 620 requires writing for immovable-property lease and registration where the term exceeds ten years; it also regulates the specified restricted lessee situation and lessee-made improvements/removal or agreed purchase.

Under s 621 the lessor can terminate for the stated rent default (including the 90-day qualification), misuse, failure to inform/restore or unapproved sublease. The lessee can terminate for unusability and the stated failures concerning repair reimbursement or rent reduction. Section 622 expressly applies **Part 4 chapter 9** to house-rent matters; this unit's general lease rules must not be used to bypass that special provision. Section 623 gives two years from accrual for chapter claims.

## 19.3 Wages: agreement, payment and safeguards

Section 641 requires proportional wages unless the person freely agreed to unpaid work; use the agreed rate or the local prevailing rate if none, and the stated completion/daily calculation defaults unless otherwise agreed. Genuine consent is essential: an employer cannot label compelled labour voluntary service. Section 642 requires adequate safety precautions. Section 643 normally limits work to eight hours per day, with consent and additional proportionate payment for extra hours under its rule; subsection (3) excepts the defined domestic-helper setting from subsection (2). That exception is not permission for abusive or unlimited child labour.

Section 645 requires undertaken work not be abandoned without reasonable grounds; it does not authorise forced labour. Section 646 pays the worker or authorised recipient, and the nearest successor if the worker dies before payment.

### Domestic helpers

Section 644 covers monthly/yearly wages, meals and suitable clothing, housing/toilet/bathing arrangements within the stated capacity qualification, study arrangements for an under-18 helper who wishes to study, treatment during illness, no work during illness or beyond age/ability/condition, funeral expenses, and no inhuman/degrading treatment or domestic violence. Treatment expenses and the specified subsistence/education expenses cannot be deducted from wages.

The Nepali s 644(3) read here **permits** written ward-committee information within one month; the unofficial English says it must be supplied. Do not silently convert that translation into a mandatory obligation. Separate requirements under other laws still need their own source.

### Working age, special law and limitation

!!! warning "Unverified"
    Civil Code s 640's checked text speaks of consent from age 14 and excludes under-16 hazardous work. It is **not a complete current permission to employ a child** or a rule of full contractual capacity. Constitution Article 39(4) prohibits child employment in factories, mines and similar hazardous work; Article 34 protects fair labour practice and lawful remuneration. Child Labour (Prohibition and Regulation) Act 2056 s 3 prohibits under-14 labour and the scheduled hazardous work for its defined child category. The Labour Act 2074, Children Act 2075 and later amendments must also be checked for the particular work. A complete reconciliation and current sector-specific minimum conditions remain unverified here; do not use s 640 alone to approve employment.

Section 647 has unusually short chapter periods: **three months for s 644 matters and 35 days for other chapter matters**, from accrual. Do not replace them with the common two-year period in several special-contract chapters, or assume they are the sole periods for a claim governed by special labour legislation.

## Full 20-mark answers

These are full practice answers. Adapt the emphasis to the question; the paragraph headings help you organise a 35-minute answer.

??? example "Q1. Explain lease and the reciprocal rights and duties of lessor and lessee. (20 marks)"
    !!! warning "Unverified"

    **Introduction**

    A lease allows one person to use another person's property for a period in return for rent. It separates ownership from the enjoyment of possession, use and benefits. Nepal's Civil Code regulates lease in sections 610 to 623. The arrangement differs from sale because ownership does not automatically pass, and from hire-purchase because paying rent does not itself complete a statutory purchase. A full examination answer should explain the property and purpose, reciprocal performance, repairs, rent, term, sublease and return. The applicable property category and any special house-rent rule must remain visible throughout.

    **Meaning and the lessor's starting duties**

    Section 610 concerns giving possession and use of property in which the lessor has the relevant right, for periodic rent during an agreed period. The definition excludes goods consumed or destroyed through their use in the stated setting. The lessor ordinarily undertakes to provide the property, ensure it is usable at transfer and arrange peaceful use, unless the agreement provides otherwise. These duties explain why rent is not simply payment for an empty legal title. The lessee contracts for the practical benefit of using the property for the lawful agreed purpose.

    A machine provided for productive use should therefore be considered in terms of whether it can perform the promised role. A dispute about a third person's interference may also affect the lessee's ability to receive the benefit. However, the precise terms and statutory qualifications matter. Not every inconvenience proves a complete failure of the lessor's obligation. The student should identify the property, the agreed use and the actual interference or defect. This gives a factual foundation for repair, rent adjustment or termination rather than treating all remedies as immediately available.

    **Prudent use and alterations**

    Section 611 restricts changing the property's original form without consent, while preserving the stated allowance for repair, maintenance, improvement or renovation. Section 612 requires good-faith, prudent use consistent with the lease purpose. A lessee cannot assume that temporary possession gives unlimited freedom to transform the property or use it for an unrelated activity. At the same time, the law does not treat every ordinary act of maintenance as a forbidden alteration. The answer should distinguish authorised maintenance from a change requiring the owner's approval and connect the use to the actual lease purpose.

    **Repair and notification**

    Section 613 ordinarily places repair and maintenance on the lessor unless otherwise arranged. Where immediate repair is necessary to make use possible, the lessee may undertake the stated work after informing the lessor, with reimbursement or adjustment in rent as provided. This protects continuity of use without authorising an unlimited expenditure programme. The necessity, notification and amount should be established. A lessee cannot simply make unrelated improvements and presume that every chosen cost must be repaid. The statutory remedy and the agreed allocation should be read together.

    Section 614 requires prompt information about damage, unfitness, claims or interference. On receiving it, the lessor has the stated fifteen-day restoration obligation, and proportional rent reduction applies where restoration cannot be completed in the statutory setting. Notification is therefore more than courtesy: it gives the lessor the opportunity and duty to respond. Section 615 separately addresses adequate protective measures and the lessee's responsibility for the stated damage, restoration and compensation, while excluding defined force majeure. The answer must preserve that exception rather than describe the lessee as an absolute insurer against every event.

    **Rent and duration**

    Section 616 starts with the agreed rent terms and provides different default dates according to the property category. It also recognises the stated proportional reduction where full possession or use is obstructed. A student should not apply one monthly deadline to every category of property without checking the provision. Rent and effective use are connected, but the right to reduce rent requires its own statutory facts. A complete refusal to pay is not automatically justified by every minor complaint. The scope of interference and the proper proportional response matter.

    Section 617 sets different maximum terms for different categories, with renewal provisions. The checked figures include thirty-five years for the specified housing and servitude land, forty for industrial or infrastructure land, twenty for farming land and nineteen for other house and land. Listed vehicle and machinery categories have fifteen years, with domestic animals and remaining goods generally ten within the stated categories. These are not a single universal limit. The relevant category and source version must be stated. A current special statute or later amendment may require separate checking before practical reliance.

    **Sublease and immovable-property formalities**

    Section 618 requires prior consent for sublease, keeps the original lessee responsible and prevents the sublease from exceeding the main term. A sublessee's direct responsibility depends on the statutory arrangement rather than an assumption that every subtenant answers every claim by the owner. Section 620 requires writing for immovable-property lease and registration where the term exceeds ten years. It also addresses the specified restricted lessee and improvements on the land. A lease document cannot be used to bypass legal restrictions on who may acquire or use particular immovable property.

    **Termination, return and conclusion**

    Section 621 identifies termination grounds for both parties. The lessor's grounds include the stated rent default, misuse, failure to notify or restore and unapproved sublease. The lessee's grounds include inability to use for the purpose and specified failures concerning repair reimbursement or rent reduction. Section 619 ordinarily requires return within thirty-five days of ending, with its stated immovable-property consequence and natural deterioration exceptions. An inventory can help determine the goods and accessories to be returned. Ending the lease does not automatically settle accrued rent or damage claims.

    Section 622 expressly applies Part 4 chapter 9 to house-rent matters, while section 623 gives two years from accrual for the lease chapter. In conclusion, lease law balances paid use with continuing ownership. The lessor must provide the promised lawful enjoyment and the lessee must pay, use prudently, communicate and return. A strong answer applies the particular category, terms and statutory qualifications, especially the house-rent rule. It does not assume that every lease is a sale, every repair is the lessee's expense or every deterioration is compensable damage.

??? example "Q2. Explain wage obligations, domestic-helper safeguards and the place of special law. (20 marks)"

    **Introduction**

    Work creates obligations that cannot be understood only as an ordinary exchange of promises between equally powerful parties. The worker depends on payment and safe conditions, while the person engaging the work expects proper performance. Nepal's Civil Code sections 640 to 647 address wages and related duties, including special provisions for domestic helpers. These rules sit within Part 5's broader law of obligations, but do not replace the Constitution or special labour and child-protection legislation. A full answer should explain the chapter and its limits, particularly where a child, domestic helper or protected employment relationship is involved.

    **Consent and the wages bargain**

    Section 641 requires payment proportionate to the work unless the worker freely agreed to work without wages. The word freely matters. An employer cannot convert compelled work into unpaid voluntary service merely by attaching a label. Where a rate was agreed, that rate supplies the chapter's starting point; otherwise the prevailing rate at the place of work applies under the provision. The section also provides the stated completion and daily-calculation defaults unless the agreement supplies another arrangement. The student should establish the work, rate, period and actual payment before calculating any amount due.

    An agreement concerning payment must still be read with mandatory law. The chapter does not prove that a rate below a legally applicable minimum is valid simply because a paper was signed. Likewise, the absence of a written price does not necessarily mean the worker undertook everything as a gift. The facts may show paid work with the statutory prevailing-rate fallback. The useful distinction is between a genuine voluntary undertaking without wages and employment whose amount was left unspecified. They are different situations and should not be collapsed into a single rule.

    **Safety, hours and performance**

    Section 642 requires adequate safety measures having regard to the work's nature and risks. Safety is therefore part of the legal obligation, not a benefit payable only if the worker negotiates it. Section 643 normally limits work under the chapter to eight hours a day. Its extra-hours rule requires consent and additional proportionate wages calculated using the stated daily basis. The provision contains a domestic-helper exception to subsection (2), but that exception cannot be expanded into permission for inhuman treatment or disregard of superior and special protective law.

    Section 645 says undertaken work should not be left incomplete without reasonable grounds and attaches responsibility to breach. That obligation does not authorise forced labour or eliminate every lawful ground for leaving unsafe or otherwise unlawful work. Section 646 directs payment to the worker or an authorised recipient and to the nearest successor where the worker dies before payment. The right to wages already earned does not simply vanish on death. A complete answer therefore addresses both performance and payment, rather than treating the worker's obligation as enforceable while the employer's is optional.

    **Domestic-helper safeguards**

    Section 644 recognises the particular setting of household work. It requires monthly or yearly wages and the stated food, clothing and living facilities subject to the capacity wording. It addresses study arrangements for an under-eighteen helper wishing to study, medical treatment during illness, protection from work while sick or beyond age, ability or condition, and the stated funeral expenses. It also prohibits inhuman or degrading treatment and domestic violence. These duties show that residence in the employer's household does not remove the helper's legal protection or turn the relationship into unlimited personal control.

    The section also restricts deductions. Medical treatment costs and the specified subsistence and education expenses cannot simply be removed from the helper's wages. A household should not promise pay and then eliminate it by charging back the very protections the law requires. The precise categories and qualifications should be stated rather than replaced by a vague claim that everything is free or everything can be deducted. In an examination example, identify the agreed wage, each proposed deduction and the relevant statutory clause. This makes the conclusion transparent and avoids unsupported arithmetic.

    **Translation and working-age cautions**

    The Nepali wording of section 644(3) read for these notes permits written information to the ward committee within one month, while the unofficial English says it must be given. The translation should not silently create a mandatory obligation absent from the checked Nepali wording. Other legislation may contain separate duties, but those need their own source. This illustrates a general method for statutory study: use translations for navigation, check the operative language and mark an unresolved interpretation rather than confidently supplying a familiar rule from another source.

    Section 640's checked text refers to consent from fourteen and excludes hazardous work below sixteen. It must not be used as a complete current permission to employ a child or as a declaration of full contractual capacity. Constitution Article 39(4) protects children against factory, mine and similar hazardous employment. Article 34 protects fair labour practice and remuneration. Child Labour Act section 3 prohibits under-fourteen labour and scheduled hazardous work for its defined child category. The Children Act, Labour Act and later changes must also be considered for the actual work and worker.

    **Special law, limitation and conclusion**

    These overlapping protections mean the Code is a study starting point, not the sole source for approving employment. The precise current sectoral regime and reconciliation of age provisions remain unverified in this unit, so the answer should not announce a universal lawful minimum age based only on section 640. The same care applies to remedies and limitation. Section 647 provides three months from accrual for section 644 matters and thirty-five days for the other chapter matters. Those periods should not be replaced with the two years found in several special-contract chapters or assumed to displace a distinct special-law claim.

    In conclusion, wage obligations combine payment, genuine consent, safety and performance, with additional domestic-helper safeguards. The chapter protects earned remuneration and prevents specified deductions and abusive treatment, while recognising that undertaken work should be completed on lawful terms. A strong answer explains these reciprocal obligations and then applies the Constitution and relevant special legislation. It avoids using a private bargain, an hours exception or an older age provision to cancel mandatory protections. That approach gives a beginner a clear statutory structure while preserving the limits of what the checked source establishes.

## One-minute revision

- Part 5 covers ss 493–691 and more than consensual contract.
- Find the general rule, then the relevant special chapter.
- Lease gives use; apply repair, rent and return provisions separately.
- House rent has the express s 622 special rule.
- Wages require genuine consent, pay and safety; domestic helpers have stated protections.
- Do not use s 640 alone for children or replace s 647 with a generic period.
